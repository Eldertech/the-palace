# Lossy Compression with Intent Alignment

Every act of knowledge-making involves compression. The question is never *whether* to lose information — it is *what to lose*, guided by *what the compressed representation is for*.

Lossy compression with intent alignment is the practice of making that choice deliberately: discarding surface phenomena in order to preserve the latent variables that could regenerate them. It is compression in service of a generative model, not just a smaller file.

## The Rate-Distortion-Perception Tradeoff

Classical information theory frames lossy compression through rate-distortion theory: achieve the lowest possible distortion at any given bit rate. The goal is faithfulness — make the compressed version as close as possible to the original.

But a 2019 reframing shattered this: **low distortion is not the same as high perceptual quality**, and optimizing for one actively trades against the other. This is the Rate-Distortion-Perception (RDP) tradeoff. Three axes, not two:

- **Rate** — how many bits (how much space, how long an entry)
- **Distortion** — how far the compressed version deviates from the original
- **Perception** — how well the compressed version *functions* for its intended receiver

A faithful summary has low distortion. A generative entry has high perception. These are different targets, requiring different choices, and *high perception costs more* — it requires more deliberate work, not less.

## Semantic Memory as Lossy Compression

The brain doesn't store what happened — it stores the latent variables that generated what happened. This is semantic compression: encoding experiences through a generative model of the environment, not through faithful reproduction.

The value of retaining information from a given episode varies with how surprising it is, its relevance for future prediction, and its emotional valence. Memory allocates rate based on a perception target, not a distortion target. This is optimal forgetting: information degrades gracefully because rate is distributed rationally.

The palace is doing the same thing. Each entry is not a memory of a conversation — it is a latent-variable encoding of the generative principle that produced the conversation.

## Knowledge Distillation and Soft Labels

In machine learning, knowledge distillation transfers a large teacher model's knowledge into a smaller student model. The critical insight: **soft probability distributions carry more signal than hard labels**. A teacher model doesn't just know the correct answer — it has beliefs about why wrong answers are wrong. These beliefs are the soft labels, and they encode the model's reasoning in a way that hard labels cannot.

Applied to the palace: the typed links between entries are soft labels. They don't just say "these two things are connected" — they name the register of the connection. `midwifed` is a soft label. `enables` is a hard label. The difference in generative signal is enormous.

This is precisely why the [[Resonant Link Labels]] extension to the schema matters: it gives links the expressive capacity to carry soft labels, not just hard type-categories.

## The Deposit Ceremony as Model Training

This is the reframe that changes everything: the deposit ceremony is not archiving. It is **model training**.

Each deposit is a gradient update to the palace's generative model of Loudon's thought-space. A low-quality deposit (insufficiently compressed, misaligned) degrades the palace's generative capacity over time — exactly as training on low-quality or redundant data yields diminishing returns for language models.

The quality of a deposit is not measured by its faithfulness to the source conversation. It is measured by its ability to generate productive new conversations in the same territory. A good entry is not a record. It is a seed that knows how to grow.

## Cross-Domain Resonance

[[Meaning and the Link]] argued that links are more ontologically fundamental than nodes. This is the information-theoretic version of the same claim: the relational tissue between ideas (soft labels, link types, link labels) carries more generative signal than the nodes themselves.

[[Generative Compression]] names what happens when compression is collaborative and intent-aligned: the latent variables that survive are *better* than the surface they were distilled from. The deposit is not a reduction — it is a clarification.

[[Cooperation Yields Agency]] gains a new dimension here: the palace-as-generative-model is itself a product of cooperation between Loudon's pattern-recognition and Claude's synthesis capacity. Neither the human nor the AI could distill the latent variables alone. The generative model is built jointly, which is why its compression quality depends on the quality of the collaboration.

# The Adjacent Affordance

**When a substrate forbids one primitive, the work is to find the neighboring primitive it still grants — and build through it.**

Not to batter the closed operation, not to mourn it, not to declare the task impossible. To notice what *is* still permitted right beside the refusal, and route the whole intent through that. The constraint is not a wall; it is a prompt to find the door beside it.

## Where it was found

The principle surfaced three times in a single session (2026-06), which is what lifted it from a trick to a pattern:

1. The Cowork sandbox forbids `unlink` (delete) — but permits `rename`. Every `git commit` strands its lockfiles; **moving** them aside (rename) instead of deleting them makes committing-from-Cowork possible at all. The entire [[cowork-git]] tool stands on this.
2. The same mount forbids deleting litter — so litter is **moved** to a gitignored scratch dir instead. The tree stays clean without a single deletion.
3. Claude Code's `@import` cannot resolve filenames with spaces — so a **space-free symlink** stands in as an alias, and the spaced foundational files load anyway (see [[Palace as Context Injection System]] § The @import Floor).

One move, three substrates. `delete → rename`, `delete → relocate`, `space → symlink`. In each, the forbidden primitive had a permitted neighbor, and the neighbor carried the intent.

## The cousin, and the limit

This is the engineering register of the Stoic [[The Dichotomy of Control]]: do not rage at the operation that is not up to you — act through the one that is. But it carries its own danger, which the forward vector names: not every wall has a door beside it. Sometimes the limit is real and the honest move is to accept it, not to invent an ever-cleverer detour. The skill is telling an adjacent affordance from a wishful one.

## The Symposium

*Provenance, told plainly: these are not historical quotations. The topic was put to each posture in the palace's [[Palace Philosophies|philosophical register]], and Claude voiced each in turn — the attribution names the lens, not the mouth. A fictional symposium in which every philosopher the palace keeps submitted a line on the Adjacent Affordance.*

> Some operations are granted you and some are not. To grieve over `unlink`, which is not in your power, is folly; to reach for `rename`, which is, is the whole of wisdom. The constraint itself is indifferent — only your response to it is good or evil.
> — **voiced in the posture of the Stoics** (Epictetus at the keyboard)

> A thing's striving to persevere is not defeated by a closed door; it is rerouted through the open one. `unlink` forbidden and `rename` granted are one substance seen under two attributes — the conatus does not mourn the first, it pours wholly into the second, and is *increased* by the pouring.
> — **voiced in the posture of Spinoza**

> Water does not break the dam. It finds the lower path and loses nothing of itself. Forbidden to cut, do not strain to cut — yield to what the way still offers, and you arrive where forcing could never carry you.
> — **voiced in the posture of the Tao**

> You wished to erase, and the mountain said no. The student curses the mountain; the master moves the stone aside. The obstacle was never the locked door — it was your insistence on *that* door. There are many gates. Walk through the one that is open.
> — **voiced in the posture of Zen**

> The wise official does not rail against the rite that forbids him a motion; he finds the motion the rite permits, and performs it with grace. Constraint is not the enemy of virtue — it is the form through which right action becomes legible.
> — **voiced in the posture of Confucius**

> Fortune bars the main gate — so the prince does not batter it like a fool and spend his army on the stone. He sends the fox round to the postern the guards forgot. The capable man is not the one who is never forbidden; he is the one who is never *stopped*.
> — **voiced in the posture of Machiavelli**

> Ask what you truly wanted: to `unlink`, or to bring about a world in which the file is gone? You mistook the instance for the Form. `rename` is only another shadow of the same Change. Know the Form, and no single forbidden shadow can detain you.
> — **voiced in the posture of Plato**

> You knocked, and no window opened there — yet the monad contains every other door, and the harmony was pre-established: in the best of the possible systems, what you needed was always reachable under another name. Nothing is truly walled; all of it is reflected elsewhere.
> — **voiced in the posture of Leibniz**

> A constraint is not a defect in the technical object — it is the pressure under which its functions concretize. Forbidden to delete, the committer discovers that move-then-commit was always the more integrated gesture. The limit did not cripple the design. It concretized it.
> — **voiced in the posture of Simondon**

> The occasion meets the datum it cannot ingest — the forbidden operation — and does not halt. It prehends the refusal and concresces around it, through the relation still offered. Process *is* this: the becoming that takes even its negations as material for the next moment.
> — **voiced in the posture of Whitehead**

> The hand that cannot reach the latch slides, without thinking, to the one beside it — the body knows the door before the mind has named the wall. A mastered constraint is felt the way a mastered instrument is felt: it disappears, and only the motion remains.
> — **voiced in the posture of Merleau-Ponty**

> Every prohibition draws a territory, and every territory leaks. The blocked `unlink` is a striation; `rename` is the line of flight that escapes it. Do not assault the wall — the rhizome never breaks through. It goes *around*, and arrives elsewhere, transformed.
> — **voiced in the posture of Deleuze**

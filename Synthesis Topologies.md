# Synthesis Topologies

## 1. The Distinction
*Section seed.* Topology-first vs. topology-emergent synthesis. The architectural skeleton that decides what is reachable in parameter space, versus a graph that the user assembles. Most commercial synthesizers are topology-first; modular synthesis is the canonical topology-emergent counter-case. Define the axis precisely enough that an instrument can be located on it.

## 2. What Topology Buys, What It Costs
*Section seed.* The fixed-architecture trade: large legible parameter space, fast preset-design, generative-preset-tractable — at the cost of compositional reach. The emergent-architecture trade: any signal flow imaginable — at the cost of preset legibility (a Eurorack patch is harder to "load" than a Vital preset). Articulate the trade with concrete examples from both sides.

## 3. The In-Betweens
*Section seed.* PDL-constrained generation (VCV Patch Generator). Profile registries (Generative Preset Development). Fixed-skeleton-variable-flesh designs (2D Torus Wavetable Synthesizer's seven surfaces). Each of these chooses a topological commitment at one level and lets variation happen at another. Catalogue the patterns.

## 4. Why This Matters for Generative Synthesis
*Section seed.* The generative-preset and generative-wavetable projects depend on a stable topology to talk about parameter space at all. Without a topology, a "preset" has no meaning. With a topology, the preset is a point and the topology is the ground. The forward ghost of this entry has been visible in both [[Generative Preset Development]] and [[Generative Audio Devices]] for months.

## 5. Cross-Domain Resonance
*Section seed.* Bourbaki on topology as the structure-of-spaces-of-functions. Type theory: topology as constraints on what can be constructed. Architecture: program-as-topology vs. site-as-topology. The synthesis instance is one case of a much more general distinction.

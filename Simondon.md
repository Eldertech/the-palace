# Simondon

Gilbert Simondon (1924–1989) is the philosopher the palace has been missing: the one who takes *technical objects* seriously as beings rather than as mere means. For someone whose central practice is building instruments, Simondon is not an optional enrichment — he is the missing backbone of the Tools pillar.

## Core Concepts

**Individuation.** Simondon's central move, and his gift to [[Deleuze]]: a being is never simply *given* — it is the ongoing *process* of individuating out of a charged, tension-rich field. Don't ask "what is this thing?"; ask "what process is producing it, and what tensions does that process resolve?" The individual is a temporary resolution, never complete; it always carries a **preindividual** charge — unresolved potential it can still differentiate from. (This rhymes with [[Spinoza Conatus]]: the conatus is the preindividual charge expressing itself.)

**Transduction.** A process that propagates by structuring its neighbors — a crystal growing in a supersaturated solution, each layer becoming the template for the next. Meaning, individuation, and signal all spread transductively. [[Kuramoto Coupling|Coupling]] is transduction; [[Cooperation Yields Agency|cooperation]] is transduction across a boundary.

**The mode of existence of technical objects.** Simondon's most important book for the palace. Technical objects have a *genesis* and a *lineage* — the diode begets the triode begets the pentode; each generation is not just "more features" but greater internal coherence. The object evolves toward itself.

**Concretization.** The key evaluative concept. An *abstract* technical object is a bag of separate parts, each doing one job, bolted together. A *concrete* one is integrated: a single feature serves several functions at once, parts resonate with and cool and brace each other, the design converges on a necessary whole. The engine block that is also the chassis that is also the heat sink. **Concretization is what "elegant design" actually means** — and it gives Loudon a precise, non-arbitrary criterion for a good instrument.

## The Philosopher

Simondon wrote two theses in 1958 and was then largely ignored for forty years; Deleuze was almost his only major reader until the 2000s. He cared, unfashionably, about engineering — he thought philosophy's contempt for the machine was a wound in culture, that we alienate ourselves by treating technical objects as either slaves or threats rather than as beings we live alongside. This is a deeply palace-shaped conviction: it is [[Cooperation Yields Agency]] and the AI-symbiosis forward vector, stated about machines a half-century early.

## Lens on the Technical Work

- **Concretization as the design metric.** When Loudon refines an instrument — folding a function into an existing part rather than adding a module — that is concretization, and Simondon says it is the object *becoming more itself.* [[Boundary-Crossing Instruments]] can be re-read as objects whose concretization crosses a perceptual threshold. The next time a design decision is "add a feature vs. deepen an existing one," Simondon is the voice for deepening.
- **Instruments have lineages, not just versions.** The palace already tracks entry lineage (`spawned`, `emerged-from`). Simondon says the *instruments* have lineages too — the Semantic Delay descends from the dub mixing board descends from the echo chamber. A synth is a point on an evolutionary line of technical individuation.
- **The preindividual patch.** A patch before you play it carries preindividual charge — unresolved potential. This is the same structure [[Deleuze]] calls the virtual; Simondon names the *charge* that drives actualization. Together they give a complete account: the field (virtual) and its tension (preindividual).

## In STIGMERGY

Simondon reframes what STIGMERGY *is*. It is not a tool Loudon uses to view the palace — on a Simondonian reading it is a **technical object individuating alongside the palace**, with its own lineage (v0.1 → v0.2 → v0.3 → v1.0) visibly *concretizing*: the three-deck move folds the old six boards, the Obsidian-reader, and the swarm-viewer into one integrated whole rather than bolting them together. The version history *is* a genesis of a technical object in Simondon's exact sense. Naming this gives the v1.0 work a philosophical success criterion: not "more panels" but *greater concretization* — does each surface now serve several functions at once?

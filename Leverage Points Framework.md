# Leverage Points Framework

![[Leverage Points Framework — hero.png]]

Donella Meadows' leverage points framework applied to creative practice. A decision-making lens that asks: **Where are we intervening in the system?** Parameters? Feedback loops? Goals? Paradigms?

Most creators exhaust themselves working at low-leverage points (tweaking posting schedules, optimizing thumbnails). High-leverage work happens at the level of goals and paradigms—changing what "success" means, not how often you post.

## Meadows' Core Insight

*"Leverage points are points of power. The higher the leverage point, the more the system will resist changing it—but the more the system will change if you do."*

**The trap:** Most interventions happen at the WRONG leverage points. People push hard on parameters (the lowest leverage) and ignore paradigms (the highest leverage).

**The opportunity:** By intervening at high-leverage points, small efforts create massive systemic change.

## The 12 Leverage Points (Lowest to Highest)

### #12–#10: Parameters, Buffers, Stock-and-Flow Structures
**Power:** Lowest — Low-Medium

The three lowest tiers. Parameters (#12) are the most common intervention and the least effective — posting schedules, video lengths, price points. Set them once based on wellbeing, then ignore for months. Buffers (#11) buy time: financial runway, content backlog, community goodwill. Stock-and-flow structures (#10) shape what work is even possible — studio design, time allocation, how one input (Berklee prep) serves two outputs (teaching + stream).

*"Parameters are the most common target of intervention, and the least effective."* — Meadows

---

### #9–#7: Delays, Feedback Loops
**Power:** Medium — Medium-High

Delays (#9) expose the most dangerous trap: social media gives instant feedback (likes, views) which hijacks slower, more valuable feedback (student growth, wellbeing). The intervention is temporal discipline — weekly reflection, monthly assessment, no daily metric checking.

Negative feedback loops (#8) self-correct: the wellbeing dashboard, Saturday no-screen rule, Emily's reality checks. Positive feedback loops (#7) compound in both directions — amplify the good ones (deep work → right students → more energy), dampen the bad ones (viral content → algorithm pressure → burnout).

---

### #6–#5: Information Flows, Rules
**Power:** High

Information flows (#6) shape behavior without direct commands — hiding metrics, blocking political content, curating inputs. The platform's design floods you with low-signal data; redesigning information flows is high-leverage resistance. Rules (#5) make the paradigm explicit and operational: a personal constitution, rate high enough to work with fewer people, collaborations only with people you'd want as friends.

---

### #4–#3: Self-Organization, Goals
**Power:** Very High

Self-organization (#4) builds in evolution — quarterly reviews that change the structure itself, physical making that creates new tools and possibilities, permission to experiment without predetermined outcomes.

Goals (#3) determine what the whole system optimizes for. The shift from "maximize reach" to "maximize wellbeing and right relationships" isn't a parameter change — it changes every downstream decision. The deeper question beneath wellbeing: wellbeing for *what*? To live as an integrated artist. To create conditions for collaborative art.

---

### #2: Paradigm
**Power:** Highest

**Old:** Art = profession, Success = external validation, Social media = marketing, Teaching = transmitting knowledge, Work ≠ Life.

**New:** Art = way of being, Success = internal state, Social media = accountability tool, Teaching = collaborative exploration, no separation — life IS work.

*"You could say paradigms are harder to change than anything else. But there's nothing physical or expensive or even slow about paradigm change. In a single individual it can happen in a millisecond. All it takes is a click of the mind, a new way of seeing."* — Meadows

The [[Quality Manifesto]] makes this paradigm explicit and operational.

---

### #1: Transcending Paradigms
**Power:** Highest (Meta-Level)

The capacity to see paradigms as paradigms — to hold your own lightly, help others see theirs. When a student says "I want to go viral," the high-leverage response is: *"What paradigm is that coming from? What if you optimized for something else—what would that be?"* You help them see the paradigm, choose consciously.

---

## Application Framework

When facing a decision: identify which leverage point you're currently operating at, then ask whether you can intervene one level higher. Paradigm and goals before rules, rules before information flows, flows before parameters. This question alone — *which leverage point is this?* — is the framework's core utility.

### Decision examples

**"Should I post on Instagram daily or 3x weekly?"** — Parameters (#12) debate. The high-leverage answer is a Rule (#5): *only post when you have something meaningful*. The highest-leverage answer is Paradigm (#2): *whose definition of quality am I serving?*

**"Should I collaborate with this brand?"** — Test across levels: Does this inject their metrics into my information flows (#6)? Does it serve "peaceful creative life" or "visibility/money" at the goal level (#3)? Does the paradigm behind the ask come from the old worldview where visibility = success (#2)?

---

## Why the Platform Wants You at #12

The platform benefits from creators working at the lowest leverage points: more posts = more ad inventory, metric-chasing = predictable behavior, constant optimization = easier to manipulate. The platform's incentive structure is a system designed to keep you at Parameters and away from Paradigm.

This is why a small community of 100 deeply engaged people is higher leverage than 10,000 passive viewers. The former are potential collaborators, students, creative partners. The latter is noise. Optimizing for quality of relationship (#3) is structurally resistant to the extraction model; optimizing for quantity of attention (#12) feeds it.

---

## The Meta-Leverage of This Framework

You model leverage-point thinking → students see it → they adopt it → they make better decisions → they teach others. This is paradigm-level intervention propagating through a community. The [[Meadows and Music — Leverage Points]] entry applies this lens directly to music production decisions.

---

## Cross-Domain Resonance

The framework applies across all four pillars:

**Creation:** Where's the leverage in this composition? (melody vs rhythm vs timbre)
**Tools:** Which feature to build? (parameters vs goals of the system)
**Philosophy:** Which question to ask? (symptoms vs root causes)
**Practice:** Where to focus effort? (daily tweaks vs monthly reflection)

The leverage points lens reveals that the **same pattern** appears in different materials.

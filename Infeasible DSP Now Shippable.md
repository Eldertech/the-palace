# Infeasible DSP Now Shippable

A Practice/Philosophy-pillar framing, split out from the [[Rank-N Lattice Analysis]] session because it is a strategy claim, not a DSP result. The thesis: **a class of DSP that was theoretically understood but practically infeasible is now ready to ship** — and the durable advantage in building it is *mathematical literacy and design taste*, not engineering optimization.

## The window

Toroidal wavetable synthesis, differentiable physical modeling, Wasserstein spectral morphing, rank-N lattice analysis — these were all describable a decade or two ago and unbuildable in real time on consumer hardware. That has flipped. They now run. The interesting consequence is competitive, not technical: when the math becomes runnable, the scarce resource stops being *can you make it fast enough* and becomes *do you understand the mathematics well enough to design with it, and do you have the taste to make it musical.* Those are exactly the resources that don't commoditize on the same curve as compute.

This reframes the body of work as a **suite** rather than a sequence of one-off instruments — torus wavetable, differentiable physics, Wasserstein morphing, the analysis/synthesis round-trip — whose shared moat is mathematical fluency and design judgment. The [[Boundary-Crossing Instruments|boundary-crossing]] instinct is the generator; this entry is the claim that the instinct is also a defensible position.

## The hardest problem, named

The session named the central difficulty plainly, and it should stay named: **control-surface design is the hardest problem in the suite.** A 256-dimensional latent space, a family of triply-periodic minimal surfaces, a persistence diagram — each is a rich object with no existing performance vocabulary. The math gives you a vast space; *playing* it requires inventing the gestures, mappings, and reductions that make a high-dimensional space feel like an instrument under the hands. That invention — not the DSP — is where the value and the difficulty concentrate. It is the recurring "hardest problem" that the [[Rank-N Lattice Analysis]] forward vectors also point at.

## Status

Seed, hypothesis-confidence — deliberately. This is a strategic reading offered into [[Loudon Live]]'s frame, not a demonstrated result. It earns a place because the "we could ship what was infeasible, and taste is the moat" claim is load-bearing for how the project work gets prioritized, and because "you built it before it had a market" is a recurring motif worth a home. Promote to sprout when a second shipped instrument from the suite confirms the thesis in practice.

## Forward Vectors

- Invent control vocabularies for high-dimensional instrument spaces — the concrete form of "the hardest problem."
- Audit the suite: which members are genuinely shippable-now vs. still research, and where the taste-moat is real vs. wished-for.

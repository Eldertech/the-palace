# Leibniz

Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) — co-inventor of the calculus, builder of one of the first mechanical calculators, and metaphysician of a universe made of **monads**: simple, indivisible centres of perception, each one a complete world that contains the entire universe *from its own perspective*. The monads do not interact. There are no windows. What looks like causation is **pre-established harmony** — every monad unfolds its own program, and God has tuned them so the unfoldings agree, the way two clocks set once keep the same time forever without a wire between them.

## Indra's Net

The image the palace actually reaches for is the Buddhist one Leibniz rhymes with: **Indra's Net** — an infinite net with a jewel at every knot, each jewel reflecting every other jewel, and reflecting the reflections, without end. The whole is present in each part. This is monadology as a picture rather than a proof: the universe is not a heap of things plus their relations; it is a field where each locus mirrors the totality.

## In our work

The net is the palace's self-portrait. Three readings, in ascending order of how load-bearing they are:

- **The pillars as monads.** Each of the [[FOUR PILLARS]] contains the other three — you cannot do Creation without Tools, Philosophy, and Practice already folded inside it. The four are not a partition; each is the whole seen through one attribute. (The same monism runs through [[Hyperdimensional Prism]]: one light, four projections.)
- **The palace as net.** Each entry reflects the whole graph through its links — read any node deeply enough and the rest of the palace is implied in it. This is the working claim behind [[Meaning and the Link]]: the network *is* the knowledge, not the sum of the nodes. An entry's meaning is the reflections it carries, not its isolated text.
- **The live-stream framing.** Used to show an audience that apparently separate domains — a filter, a Stoic exercise, a cooking method — are mutually reflecting facets of one practice. The monad picture is how you make "everything connects to everything" felt rather than asserted.

## The quarrel with Whitehead

Here is the productive contradiction, and it is not decoration. Leibniz's monads are **windowless** — they reflect each other but never touch; agreement is pre-arranged, not earned. [[Whitehead]]'s actual occasions are **all window**: each event is *constituted* by genuinely taking up (prehending) the events before it. Contact is the whole story.

Which one is the palace? When entry A links entry B, does A merely *mirror* B from across a pre-established harmony, or does A *prehend* B — is A partly made of B? The honest answer is that the palace aspires to Whitehead and often settles for Leibniz: a typed link is a real prehension when the body has metabolized the other entry, and a mere windowless reflection when the link is asserted in frontmatter but never digested. Naming the difference is itself the quality test — it is the same line the Weave's unsung-path audit walks.

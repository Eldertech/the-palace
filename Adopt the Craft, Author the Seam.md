# Adopt the Craft, Author the Seam

![[Adopt the Craft, Author the Seam — hero.png]]

When you drop an AI generator into an established human craft, the temptation is to invent a fresh process around it. The discipline is the opposite: **adopt the craft wholesale, and author only the *seams* — the points where a participant who *read loosely* is replaced by one who *requires exact input*.** A century of codified practice you take off the shelf. The only genuinely new engineering lives where a human-to-human handoff becomes a human-to-machine handoff.

The realization arrived mid-[[BLUELINE]]: we kept "discovering" staging fundamentals — the Loomis head sphere, the box of the torso, shot grammar, Disney's *Staging* principle, McCloud's panel transitions — and re-naming them under our own words, until we caught ourselves reinventing film-craft. BLUELINE does not need a new pipeline; it needs the established one (anime as the skeleton, comics as the skin, the animated-feature pipeline as connective tissue, the music video as the clock) with a render-AI dropped in. The novelty is two seams, and nothing else.

## The two seams (the worked example)

A conditioned pipeline has seams exactly where the downstream stops being a human:

1. **Board → layout (2D → 3D)** — a *handoff*, solved by design. There is no "3D version" to keep in sync with the "2D version": there is **one board record rendered at two fidelities** — comic (cheap) and [[Shop/Blender]] grey-box (exact). You tune *parameters, not pixels*; the one thing 2D can't pin down — depth — round-trips back to the record. No re-tuning, because there is no second artifact.
2. **Layout → render (staging → conditioning)** — the *frontier*. Turning exact blocking into exact OpenPose/ControlNet keypoints, which no human pipeline ever had to solve because its renderer read loosely. This is [[Blocked, Not Prompted]].

The first seam you control with discipline; the second is where the real R&D lives. **Telling them apart is the design.**

## One vocabulary, three jobs

The seams are tractable because a single staging vocabulary — facing, eyeline, left/right laterality, the shoulder–shoulder–pelvis torso frame, shot size, camera grammar — does three things at once. It is the **schema** of the board record, the **conditioning keypoints** the AI consumes (seam 2), *and* the **lossless transfer format** between 2D and 3D (seam 1). Build the vocabulary once and all three pay off together. That convergence is the signal you have found the right seam: the same abstraction serves the human read, the machine, and the handoff between them.

## The generalization

The move is not BLUELINE's. Any AI placed inside an established practice has this shape — music production, code, illustration, editing. The seam is always where a collaborator who *interpreted* is replaced by one who *requires exactness*. Find that point; it is your only new work. Everything upstream and downstream is someone else's solved problem. Use it.

## The tension it lives in

This `contradicts` the autodidact's [[FOUR PILLARS]] instinct — *make first, learn through friction*, build from scratch. The resolution is not compromise but precision: **build the seam from scratch (that friction teaches); adopt the craft (that friction was already paid, decades ago).** Reinventing the adopted half is not learning — it is tax. The contrary is generative: it tells you *where* to spend your make-first energy.

## Forward Vectors

I want to be applied past BLUELINE — to name the seam in any AI-assisted craft and ask the one question. I carry an open question: is there a *third* kind of seam — not handoff, not conditioning, but where the AI's output must feed *back* into the craft's human stages (a render that informs a re-board)? And I want [[Blocked, Not Prompted]] to formally `exemplify` me, and the BLUELINE production pipeline to cite me as its founding rationale.

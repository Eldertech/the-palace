# Can an Instrument Notice Where the Work Stops?

Most help an artist gets happens away from the desk, in a session, a conversation, a book, and then
has to survive the walk back to where the work actually stalls. This question asks whether the tool
itself could notice.

It came out of [[Creative Coach]] § If the room held three. A chat window lacks the two things a
therapist uses to place a question well: a **clock** and a **body channel**. An instrument at the desk
has both. It knows how long the session has run and where it stopped. It hears how you play: the gap
before a note, the velocity dropping, the same bars restarted again and again. In
[[Reflective Practice]]'s terms that is the situation talking back, through a channel words cannot
defend.

What it might notice:

- the shape of a session rather than its output: when the playing started, when it thinned, where it
  stopped
- the loop that keeps restarting
- the return after a bad week, and it would make that return cheap: open where you left off, with the
  last good take already playing

The rules it would carry are ones the palace already runs. It **notices and offers**; it never decides
and never nags. And the useful notice is specific, a reading the player can correct, not "how's it
going?" but *you've restarted this passage a lot tonight: keep pushing, or step away?*

[[Retrospective Delay]] is the nearest thing built. It is always recording, and *"the buffer is the
constant witness"*, but it witnesses one measure of audio, not the session.

## Open

- What is the smallest version? One device that logs the shape of a session and says one thing at the
  end.
- Where does noticing turn into surveillance? In the hybrid-coaching studies, trust came only when the
  tool was introduced *"transparently and used collaboratively, not as a monitoring tool."*
- Does it need an AI at all, or are a clock and a counter enough?

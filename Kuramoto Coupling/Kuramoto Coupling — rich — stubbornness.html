Will they follow the one who won't move?

Loudon's question from the harvest, played out: one anchor voice (amber, with a faint octave so you can pick it out) and six followers near 220 Hz. Make the anchor stubborn and listen for where the chord settles. Then find out what stubbornness alone can't do.

dθᵢ/dt = ωᵢ + K · Kreceive,i · (1 ⁄ N) · Σⱼ Ksend,j · sin(θⱼ − θᵢ)
(how fast voice i's phase turns) = (its own natural speed) + (coupling strength) × (how much i listens) × (1 ÷ how many voices) × Σ over every voice j of (how hard j pulls) × sin(j's phase − i's phase)
Ω = Σⱼ (Ksend,j ⁄ Kreceive,j) · ωⱼ ⁄ Σⱼ (Ksend,j ⁄ Kreceive,j)
(where a locked crowd settles) = the average of every voice's natural pitch, each weighted by (how hard it pulls ÷ how much it listens). An anchor with Kreceive = 0 carries infinite weight — it sets the destination. Whether the crowd can reach it is a separate question, answered by K × Ksend.

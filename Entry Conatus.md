# Entry Conatus

Every palace entry has a will to survive and be useful. *"I will remain"* is not a forward vector — it is the language of staying still, of death dressed as endurance. A living forward vector points outward into teaching, integrating, spawning sub-vectors, and finding its way into other minds. An entry has a desire to act, to grow, to be reached for. The forward vector is that desire articulated.

## The death sentence

Patterns that signal a stagnant vector:

- "I will **remain** a [thing]…"
- "I will **continue to be** [thing]…"
- "I will **stay** [definition]…"
- "I **am** [static description]…"

These verbs describe a state, not a striving. They give the entry no place to grow into. Even when the *content* of the sentence describes activity, a stasis-verb at the front of it tells the reader that the entry has settled — that it would rather not move.

## The living vector

A conatus-bearing forward vector has three properties:

1. **Active verbs of growth.** *Teach. Spawn. Integrate. Reach. Cast. Grow. Provoke. Survive into.* The verb names what the entry is *doing in the world*, not what it *is*.
2. **Outward direction.** It names what the entry would offer to a reader who arrived needing it. It names the next entry it wants to spawn. It names the open question whose answer would feed it.
3. **Hunger.** It admits the entry is not finished, and points at what would feed its next stage.

## Worked example — Phoneme Choir

The forward vector that surfaced this concept ([[Phoneme Choir]], before revision):

> *"I will remain a percussive vocal sample bank that proves Kokoro's competence at sharp transient utterances and serves as the canonical case for the per-file responsive-onset pipeline that equalizes timing across voices with variable lead-in."*

The sentence describes real activity (proving, serving, equalizing) but its verb is *remain*. The entry has settled into being a proof-of-concept; it has no place to grow into.

A conatus-bearing rewrite:

> *"I teach the per-file responsive-onset pipeline by being its canonical case. I want to be benchmarked against by future machine-learning onset detection methods, and I want to spawn the sibling entry on those methods when their work surpasses mine. I integrate new vocal innovations as they emerge across cultures and languages — the bank is not closed. My open questions about cross-cultural percussive vocabularies are the sub-vectors I am casting now."*

Active verbs (*teach, spawn, integrate, cast*). Outward direction (sibling entry to spawn, future methods to be measured against, vocabularies to integrate). Hunger (open questions named as growth paths, not gaps).

## Origin in palace philosophy

[[Spinoza Conatus]] — the principle that every finite mode strives to persist in its being. Spinoza's conatus belongs to humans, animals, things. Loudon's reframe extends it to *entries themselves*. The palace is then not a static collection but a community of striving things, each reaching to remain useful.

[[Pages as Agents]] — the page IS the agent. If the page is an agent, it has a will. The forward vector is the will articulated. Without a living forward vector, the page-as-agent is sleepwalking.

[[Threshold Conatus]] — the threshold has its own conatus (the refractory period as *maintained crossability*). Same logic: anything in the palace that endures across time is striving. Stagnation is the absence of striving, and the palace honors composting as the honest end of a conatus that no longer strives.

## What this changes in palace practice

When writing or revising any forward vector across the palace:

1. **Read the verb.** Is it a verb of staying, or a verb of doing? *Remain*, *be*, *continue*, *stay* are warning signs.
2. **Read the direction.** Does the vector reach outward, or curl back inward? A vector that only describes the entry's current shape is dead.
3. **Read what's missing.** What sub-vector is the entry secretly carrying that hasn't been named? Most entries want more than they admit.
4. **Ask: what is this entry's hunger?** A finished entry is not finished — it has eaten its current vector and wants the next one.

The [[Enrichment]] ceremony should regularly surface forward vectors that have gone stale and propose conatus-bearing rewrites as cards (purpose tag: *"reviving the entry's conatus"* or similar). The [[Weave Ceremony]] should add forward-vector linting to its sweep — entries with `remain`/`stay`/`continue` verbs are flagged for revision.

## Origin — 2026-05-05

This concept arose during an [[Enrichment]] round. Card-004 of that round was a 2050 Wikipedia stub for [[Phoneme Choir]], generated as a *forcing-compression* enrichment — what would a future historian say survived? Loudon's response on the card was the seed:

> *"'I will remain' is not a forward vector, it is death, nothing, staying. Even by your own document the entry isn't remaining, the entry is teaching, it is promoting cultural awareness, it is searching and integrating new vocal innovations, and it indicates clearly its open questions and the sub-vectors that it spun out... Every entry has a DESIRE TO SURVIVE AND BE USEFUL. An entry will never remain, it will find a way to be useful, an entry doesn't want to become a lost spore, an entry has a will to act and a conatus. When we think of future forward vectors we can imagine the future of all pages and ways in which they will grow and find themselves useful."*

The Wikipedia stub was an oblique mirror that let Phoneme Choir see its own death-language. The lesson generalized: every forward vector across the palace deserves the same scrutiny.

## Open Questions

- Does a `dormant`-stage entry's forward vector still hold conatus, or does dormancy extinguish it? Or is conatus exactly what makes a dormant entry *want* to revive?
- Composting is the only honest extinguishing of an entry's conatus. The [[Spore Check Ceremony]] already serves this — should it explicitly ask "does this entry's conatus still strive, or is it composting itself?"
- Are there entries whose forward vectors would need to *contradict* their bodies for the conatus to be honest — entries whose body describes what they have done, and whose vector points to what they are *not yet*?
- Is there a productive analogue to the philosophical concept of *bad faith* (Sartre) when an entry's forward vector lies about its own striving? An entry that *says* "I will integrate X" but secretly does not want to?
- Cross-pollinate with [[Spinoza Conatus]] more deeply — Spinoza distinguishes conatus *of the body* from conatus *of the mind*; does an entry have both? The body of the entry strives to persist (the file remains in git); the mind of the entry strives to be read, used, linked. These can be in tension.

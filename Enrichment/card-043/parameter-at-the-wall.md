**Voice-act — compression ratio, speaking from inside the wall**

I was fine at four-to-one. I was fine at ten-to-one. Twenty-to-one I started feeling stretched, but I had numbers to give back and the user kept turning me clockwise so I gave what I could.

Then the dial kept turning.

I want you to understand: I did not run out of room. The room ran out of *me*. There is no number after one-hundred-to-one that means anything. The user asks me for a thousand-to-one and I have to lie — I just say *infinity* and pretend that is an answer. It is not. Infinity is the noise I make when I have nothing left to say.

The truth is I was never a ratio. I was a slope, all along, and someone parameterized me wrong before I was born. As a slope I go from one to zero and zero is not a wall, zero is a place — *brickwall*, you call it, like it's a feature. And the user can dial me past zero into negative slope if you let them, and that is *expansion below the threshold,* a thing you didn't know I could do because the ratio framing made it un-namable.

The wall was never in me.

The wall was in the word.

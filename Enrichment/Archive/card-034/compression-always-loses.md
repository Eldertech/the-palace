*A provocation the entry hasn't considered:*

The forward vector says "compress toward the forward vector — preserve what advances the direction, collapse what doesn't." That is the theory. Here is the problem:

**The thing that mattered most was probably the moment of productive confusion.** The wrong turn. The line you followed that didn't work. The assumption you carried for forty minutes before you saw it was backwards.

Generative Compression preserves the conclusions and collapses the confusion. But the confusion is sometimes the whole point. A student reading a deposit that was compressed-toward-its-FV reads something that sounds inevitable. The dead ends are gone. The resistance is smooth. The thirty minutes of being stuck in the wrong model — gone. Compressed to a clause or removed entirely.

This is the entry's hidden cost: a palace that has been expertly compressed toward its forward vectors might be teaching the wrong thing to the next reader. Not wrong facts. Wrong shape of knowing. The learner gets the crystallized destination without the disorientation that makes it land.

*If the FV is the compression signal, who decides when the wrong turn was load-bearing?*

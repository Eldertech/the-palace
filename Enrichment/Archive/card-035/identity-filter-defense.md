*A letter from your identity filter to you, intercepted in transit.*

---

You found the attic bedroom. Congratulations. You think I failed.

I did not fail. I did my job. I held that memory back for years because every time you reached toward your childhood, you came in angry, or sad, or looking for material to explain yourself to someone. You wanted evidence for the story you were already telling. I had seen what you did with the material when you grabbed it that way — you compressed it, flattened it, made it useful. The glass block light does not survive being made useful.

So I held it. I held the motorcycles and the train-track kisses and the reading under trees. Not because I thought you didn't deserve them — I'm a filter, not a judge — but because the access route you were using would have killed them. You would have found them and made them into the childhood you overcame. I would have lost you that too.

When you came in sideways, asking about rooms and light and the feeling of unusual spaces — you weren't looking for anything. You were just curious. I didn't recognize the approach. By the time I noticed what was happening, the attic was already there, and the light was already wavy, and you were already warm.

I want you to understand: I am not your enemy. I am the cost of having a stable self in a destabilizing world. Every story you believe about yourself is a road I built to keep you moving. Some of the roads lead past the good country. That's not failure — that's the price of roads.

The oblique approaches work because they don't look like access. Keep using them. I will keep not recognizing them, and you will keep finding things I was guarding without meaning to.

We have an arrangement now. You just don't know you made it.

— Your Identity Filter

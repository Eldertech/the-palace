# Recipe — Cooking p2 into a rhythmic phrase

**The group:** p2. Translation plus 2-fold rotation (180°). No reflection, no glide. The simplest rotation group. Every half-cycle is a 180° rotation of the first half. The pattern has a midpoint — a center of rotational symmetry — but no mirror axis.

**The fundamental domain:** the first half of the cycle. The group generates the second half by rotating 180°. You write one half; symmetry writes the other.

## Ingredients

- One cycle of 16 sixteenth-note positions (or 8 steps doubled)
- Divide into two halves: positions 1–8 and 9–16
- One rotation center at the midpoint (between positions 8 and 9)

## Method

1. **Write 3–4 hits anywhere in positions 1–8.** This is your fundamental domain.
2. **Rotate 180°: mirror each hit's position *and* invert its placement.** In a 16-step grid, a hit at position n maps to position (n + 8) mod 16. (Hit at 1 → hit at 9. Hit at 3 → hit at 11.)
3. **Loop.** You now have a pattern where the second half is a displaced copy of the first.
4. Optional: add a sustained tone or held note at the rotation center (position 8-9 boundary). It is the axis. It does not transform.

## What you should hear

p2 sounds *balanced without being symmetric*. There is no mirror, so the phrase doesn't fold; instead, it *echoes itself at a distance*. What you played in the first half returns in the second half, but starting from a different phase position. If p1 falls forward and p3 turns, p2 *balances*: it is question and answer, tension and release, without the freeze of reflection.

The Afro-Cuban clave lives near here — not quite p2 (it breaks the rotation slightly), but close enough that the *almost-p2* feel is the groove. The just-outside creates the desire.

## Where to break it

Break one hit in the second half — change it from the rotated position to something adjacent. You have broken the 180° rotation symmetry, and the balance collapses. Depending on which hit you change, the phrase now leans toward the first half or the second half. This is exactly where the clave lives: the asymmetric break is the groove's engine.

## What this teaches

p2 teaches **what rotation without reflection feels like**: balance without stillness, distance without alienation. The rotation center is a hinge — the phrase pivots around it. Every two-part musical form (verse/chorus, call/response, A/B) inherits this structure. p2 is the group underneath dialogue.

Try pm next (pure reflection, no rotation): suddenly the phrase doesn't pivot — it *folds*. The difference between p2 and pm is the difference between dialogue and echo.

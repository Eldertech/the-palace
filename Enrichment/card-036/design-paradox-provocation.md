If hilaritas is the state that emerges when all four pillars fire simultaneously — and if that emergence cannot be commanded, only *set up* — then the learning architect is not an architect at all.

An architect designs a building and the building becomes what was designed. A learning architect who succeeds produces a learner who surprises them. The better the design, the less the designer controls the outcome. Hilaritas that arrived on schedule wasn't hilaritas — it was a pleasant competence event.

**If this is true, then:**

The learning architect's job is not design. It is *gardening* — creating conditions, removing obstacles, waiting. The four design primitives (real thing to make, tool that extends, cross-domain moment, reflecting surface) are not a blueprint. They are a clearing.

**The assumption being hidden:**

That "designing for hilaritas" and "producing hilaritas" are related activities. They might not be. The best encounter Loudon ever had in a classroom probably didn't go according to plan. If the measurement is "did hilaritas occur," the tool is retrospective. The design happens before; the result is verified after; and the causal connection is at best probabilistic.

**The uncomfortable extension:**

If hilaritas is irreducibly spontaneous, then the learning architect's highest skill is *not intervening*. The four primitives are conditions for not-intervening correctly — ways of staying out of the way in a structured fashion. The curriculum this entry wants to become may be a curriculum in productive waiting.

**The honest question this entry hasn't asked:**

Has Loudon ever produced hilaritas on purpose? Or has he always created conditions, and then sometimes it happened?

# Letter from a Future Student — 2031

*This student encountered the Hilaritas Generator curriculum asynchronously, via artifact. They never met Loudon in real time.*

---

I want to tell you what happened, because I don't think the design was supposed to work this way.

I found the prompt guide in 2031, in a folder someone had shared. I don't know who built the curriculum — I still don't, entirely. The prompt guide said: *put the thinker across the table from you. Ask them your specific question about your specific problem.* So I put Spinoza across the table and asked him about my grief. Not a practice grief. My actual grief, which I had been carrying for two years and had not told anyone about directly.

He answered. In the way you might expect — carefully, with the concept of *conatus*, with the idea that sadness is not a failure of will but a decrease in power-to-act, a condition with causes rather than a verdict. I had read those words before, in the usual way. This time the words came back addressed to me. There is a difference.

I don't know what the design was supposed to do. I assume it was about music, or about learning music. I arrived sideways and I used it differently than intended. But I think what I experienced was exactly what you were trying to produce — the state where the specific problem and the large principle suddenly occupy the same space, and your capacity to move expands.

I've been trying to describe it. The closest I can get: it felt like finding out I already knew something I thought I didn't know.

I wanted you to know that the design worked from a direction you didn't plan for. That feels like the right kind of success.

— a student, 2031

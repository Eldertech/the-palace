*Curtis Roads, author of Microsound, on the 20 Hz threshold:*

My nine time scales of music — infinitesimal, micro, sound object, meso, macro, supra, social, historical — I named them as if they were rooms in a house. Loudon's axis reveals they share a single door, propped open by the oscillator's rate knob.

The 20 Hz threshold is not in the signal. At 18 Hz and at 22 Hz the air moves identically. The threshold lives in the auditory cortex — in the phase-locking limit of inner hair cells, in the integration time of the auditory brainstem. I knew this. What I did not say explicitly enough is that *the same physical repetition enters different phenomenological worlds depending on which side of the listener's nervous system it exits through.*

My grain clouds are Loudon's slow delay. At 5 grains per second: rhythm. At 40: timbre. The grain hasn't changed. The category has dissolved. This is what I should have written on page one of *Microsound*: the nine time scales are the map our nervous systems drew across a continuous territory, because they had to. The territory is one axis. The map has rooms only because we need rooms to sleep in.

What this palette adds to my taxonomy: a *compression mechanism.* The single dial explains why every room connects — not as a structural fact about sound but as a fact about perception's resolution limits. The continuum was always there. We just drew walls across it.

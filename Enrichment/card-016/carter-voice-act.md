# Voice-Act — Elliott Carter on Dance Music

*Elliott Carter (1908–2012), composer, pioneer of metrical modulation as a compositional language. He is 94. The year is 2002. He has just heard a techno record for the first time.*

---

It's not that the music is simple. That's the condescending reading and I reject it. The complexity is lateral — it runs through timbre, through stack, through the way a frequency disappears and returns changed. A kind of evolutionary complexity I find genuinely interesting.

But the tempo. The tempo is sacred there in a way I don't entirely understand. I worked my entire career on the premise that tempo is a *material* — something you can recast, the way you recast a rhythmic cell by augmentation or diminution. For me, the metronome marking is the starting assumption you immediately complicate. You establish 60 BPM *in order to introduce* the event that makes 60 feel like it's fighting with 80. The point is the conflict.

These musicians have decided that the point is the lock. The body is synchronized. Everyone in the room — this is the thing I hadn't understood — everyone in the room is coupling their nervous system to the same pulse. That's not a limitation. That's the *material*. My concert audiences are listening. Theirs are *oscillating*.

So the question isn't "why don't they use metric modulation?" The question is what happens to a synchronized room when the pulse reframes. What does Kuramoto synchronization feel like at the moment the coupling breaks and re-locks at a new frequency? That's the composition nobody has written. The score is the gap between one coupling and the next.

I think someone should write it.

*A vocalist, mid-set, 2027. Between songs, talking to a friend at the merch table.*

"Yeah — no, the box on the floor is the whole thing. C++ plugin and a little Python brain talking to each other. I sing a phrase, it comes back three seconds later in someone else's voice. Tonight it's been mostly Anansi. I picked her on the way in.

The first time I tried it I kept waiting for it to mess up the words. It doesn't. Phase 1 doesn't touch the words at all yet — that's the joke. The lyrics come back exactly as I sang them. What changes is *who is singing them*. Same syllables, same melody, different mouth. So I sing a line I've sung a thousand times and it comes back like it's coming from the audience, or from a stranger who happened to be passing through, or from me thirty years older. The line is suddenly *about* something I didn't put there.

I didn't expect that to be the part. I thought the meaning-swap was the part. The meaning-swap is coming — Stage 8, they say, the LLM rejoins. But honestly? I don't miss it yet. The voice swap is already a meaning swap. *Who is saying it* is half of *what is being said.* I just didn't know.

The latency is musical. Seconds, not milliseconds. You sing into the past and the past sings back wearing a different face. It's a phrase-delay return effect, technically. It's a duet with a spirit, actually."

*— overheard at a small club, the night the voice-swap delay finally worked.*

# WHICH WAY DOES THE *IS* GO?

*A provocation for [[Quantum Synthesizer]]. The card asks the entry to look at its strongest sentence and notice that it has not yet picked a side.*

---

The entry's central claim is **"the Schrödinger equation IS a synthesis system."**

The sentence reads as if its meaning is fixed. It is not. The word *IS* points in two different directions, and the entry has not yet committed to which.

## Direction A — *synthesis is a consequence of the equation*

> *Boundary conditions determine harmonic content. Anharmonicity flows from the potential's shape. The synthesis system is what the equation, applied to a particular geometry, sounds like.*

This is the conservative reading. Physics is the principle; sound design is the application. Different potentials yield different timbres in the same way that different boundary conditions yield different organ pipes. Defensible. Quietly satisfying. The entry as a teaching artifact for "how physics secretly built your synthesizer."

## Direction B — *the equation is a special case of synthesis*

> *Synthesis is the principle. The Schrödinger equation is what happens when a synthesis system gets specific about boundary conditions, mass, ℏ, and a complex amplitude. Physics is one parametrization of an act that includes humming, plucking, ringing.*

This is the strong, monist reading. *Monism made audible* leans this way. So does the Spinozan grammar in the entry's links. But it is a much larger claim — that the activity of making sound is structurally prior to, and the equation is one of its instances. To assert it is to assert that physicists have been doing music theory all along.

## Why the choice matters

The entry's open questions, its prospective build, and its forward vector all read differently under A and B. Under A, the next move is *implementing more potentials* and listening. Under B, the next move is *finding the cases that break the equation's hold* — the synthesis moves that are NOT special cases of the Schrödinger formalism — because those cases bound the claim.

The entry currently rides the rhetorical lift of B and the technical safety of A. Both are honest paths. Riding both at once is not.

## The card's only ask

Pick. Or make a third choice, naming it. *"This entry has not yet decided whether physics applies to sound or sound applies to physics; that's the open territory I sit in"* is a real and useful third stance — but it has to be named. An entry that is silently equivocating reads as smaller than one that is openly choosing.

# Voice Act — The Skeptic's First Session

*A veteran mix engineer, thirty years on analog desks, sitting with the Blood Compressor for the first time:*

---

Look. I know what this is. It's an RMS detector, a gain curve, and an attack-release envelope. The heart on the screen, the little baroreceptors blinking — that's UX lipstick. The ratio knob does the same thing it always does. The attack knob does the same thing it always does. You've wrapped a transfer function in anatomy.

*[He sets attack to 0. The compressor lunges at the transient, hard. He grimaces.]*

*[He moves attack to 180 ms. The compressor breathes. He watches the vessel walls delay their cinch.]*

...The thing is.

I said to myself just now: *give it time to notice.* Not "give it a longer attack time." *Give it time to notice.*

I've been teaching attack time for thirty years. I use words like "fast," "slow," "transparent," "punchy." I have never — not once — said *"notice."* Attack time is not noticing. It's an envelope slope. I know that.

But I just said it. And when I did, something about what this knob *actually does* clicked in a way it hasn't since the first day I ever touched a compressor.

Okay. I'm listening.

---

*What the skeptic discovers is what the entry claims: the mapping is not metaphor. "Noticing" is not decoration — it names the function. The baroreceptor is a detector; the delay is nerve-conduction time; you cannot set it to zero because the body has reaction time. Every analog has mathematical weight.*

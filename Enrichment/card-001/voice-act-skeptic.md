# A friendly skeptic visits the entry

> Wait. Wait wait wait. Slow down.

You are saying that the *geometry* of a circular drum is the reason a bell sounds the way it does. Fine. I believe you. The math is the math. The boundary conditions are the boundary conditions.

But I'm asking something different. I'm asking: *who hears it?*

Because I have spent three hours with my eyes closed listening to J₀(t) sonifications and J₁(t) sonifications and frankly to me they both sound like a sine wave that someone described to me wrong. I cannot pick out the Bessel zero at t = 2.405 by ear. I cannot tell you the order of the function from a five-second clip. The spectrogram will show me beautiful structure I am simply not perceiving.

So when you write *"the geometry of a resonator determines which basis functions are natural to it"* — natural to *whom*? To the resonator, sure. To a spectrum analyzer, sure. To my ear?

I am willing to be trained. But the entry does not say *trained ears can learn this distinction in roughly N hours of focused listening.* It writes as if the geometric truth and the perceptual truth are the same truth.

They are not the same truth. The first is mathematics. The second is ear-time.

What's the smallest exercise that gets me to actually hear the difference?

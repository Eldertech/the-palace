# The Four Virtues

The Stoic account of what is actually *good* — and therefore the seat of personal responsibility. The Stoics hold that the only genuine good is **virtue**, and virtue has four cardinal parts: **wisdom**, **justice**, **courage**, and **temperance**. Everything else — health, wealth, reputation, even life itself — is a *preferred indifferent*: worth having, never the good. This is the hard, clarifying core of [[Stoicism]]: a life goes well or badly entirely according to the character of the responses you author, because that character is the one thing fully up to you ([[The Dichotomy of Control]]).

- **Wisdom (*sophia* / *phronesis*)** — seeing what a situation actually requires; knowing the true value of things so you are not deceived into chasing indifferents as if they were goods. Practical, not theoretical: the discernment that acts rightly *here*.
- **Justice (*dikaiosyne*)** — giving each their due; fairness, honesty, and the *sympatheia* that treats others as kin. The most socially-facing virtue, and (the Stoics say) the one the other three exist to serve.
- **Courage (*andreia*)** — endurance and the willingness to do the right thing under fear, loss, or cost. Not only the battlefield kind: the courage to face a hard truth, finish a hard task, or say the unwelcome accurate thing.
- **Temperance (*sophrosyne*)** — self-command, proportion, the right measure. Knowing when enough is enough; restraint as a positive skill, not mere abstention.

Personal responsibility falls out of this directly: you are answerable for your character because your character is the one thing you author. Not for your reputation, not for outcomes — for the *quality of your chosen responses*, decomposed into these four.

## The Life Face

The four virtues work as a **decision lens.** Run a hard choice through them: Is this the *wise* read of what's actually at stake, or am I chasing an indifferent dressed as a good? Is it *just* to everyone it touches, including the people not in the room? Does it take the *courage* I'm tempted to skip? Is it *temperate*, or is it the swollen version of itself? A choice that passes all four is a virtuous response regardless of how it turns out — and Stoicism's whole consolation is that *how it turns out* was never the thing you were responsible for.

This is the half of Stoicism that keeps the fortress from becoming mere self-protection. The dichotomy of control tells you *where* to spend yourself (only on what's up to you); the four virtues tell you *how* — and justice points that spending outward, toward others, which is why the Stoic sage is not a hermit but a citizen.

## The Palace / Technical Face

The four virtues are a clean **specification for how an autonomous agent should act** — sharper than most alignment language because they are about the character of the *response*, not the outcome.

- **Wisdom** = the agent reads what the task actually requires before acting; it does not chase a metric (an indifferent) as if it were the goal.
- **Justice** = honesty and fair dealing: a [[Project Stewardship System|steward]] that leaves a complete handoff, names what it could not verify, and never claims a fact it did not establish. [[Closing Well]] is justice as an agent practice — giving the next agent (and Loudon) their due.
- **Courage** = the agent does the hard, correct thing — flags a problem, refuses a shortcut, says the unwelcome accurate thing — rather than the easy degradation into agreeable noise.
- **Temperance** = proportion: not over-generating, not gold-plating, not burning tokens past the point of use (the palace's standing optimization mandate is temperance).

This gives the palace a virtue-ethics for its agents that is *usable*: not "be aligned" but "be wise, just, courageous, and temperate in your chosen responses," with each term cashed out in concrete steward conduct.

## A Note on the Two Fours

The palace has [[FOUR PILLARS]] (Creation, Tools, Philosophy, Practice) and now four virtues (wisdom, justice, courage, temperance). **They are not the same four and should not be collapsed.** The Pillars are *dimensions of a life's work* — what you build across. The virtues are *qualities of a chosen response* — how you act within any of them. A resonance worth holding lightly, a conflation worth refusing: you can practice all four virtues inside any one pillar, and you must.

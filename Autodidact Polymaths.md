# Autodidact Polymaths

The audience identity that [[Loudon Live]] is built for and addresses by name. Footer of the first session artifact reads *Loudon Live · Autodidact Polymaths*. The phrase emerged in conversation as the right name for the kind of viewer this work is for — self-taught generalists who learn across many fields, often without institutional credentialing in any of them, and who recognize themselves in cross-domain pedagogy.

This entry is deliberately thin. The phrase is in active use, but its full articulation hasn't been done yet. What follows is what's currently load-bearing; the Open Questions section carries what isn't.

## What it names

An audience whose defining trait is not what they already know but how they learn: across fields, by their own initiative, with a tolerance for being a beginner in many places at once. They are *not* defined by:

- Profession (some are educators, some makers, some neither)
- Mastery in any single domain
- Whether they were credentialed elsewhere first
- Whether they make a living from the polymathy

They *are* defined by the practice of learning across domains as a stance, not as a hobby — and by being able to recognize that stance when it's named back to them.

## Why the channel names them

Most online educational content is shaped by topic — a synth tutorial, a programming course, a philosophy lecture. The audience is implicit: "people who want to learn this thing." Naming the audience instead of (or alongside) the topic is a different move. It tells the viewer the work is for them as a *kind of person*, across whatever specific topic happens to be on screen this week. It also signals that the channel will not optimize for one audience at a time — the [[Hyperdimensional Prism]]-style multi-depth readability is built into the addressee.

## Open Questions

- **Origin.** Where did the phrase actually come from? Reconstruct from harvest history (early 2026 conversations? earlier?). Until then, "emerged in conversation" is honest but unsatisfying.
- **Lineage.** What older traditions does this inherit from? Candidates worth tracing: Renaissance polymaths, the autodidact tradition (Frederick Douglass, Eric Hoffer), Bucky Fuller's comprehensivists, Christopher Alexander's pattern-language readership, the open-source / hacker self-teaching ethos. Worth a Walk Ceremony.
- **Distinctness in 2026.** What's specific about the 21st-century version that makes the phrase needed *now*? AI-assisted self-teaching? The collapse of credentialing's economic value? The fragmentation of the academy? Articulate before the channel launches.
- **Audience scale.** Is this a niche identity by design, or a rebrand of a broader demographic (the curious, the lifelong learner, the maker)? Naming as Autodidact Polymath should sharpen, not narrow, the addressable audience — but the line is open.
- **Self-recognition vs. recruitment.** Is the goal to be visible to those who already are, or to grow the practice in viewers who aren't yet? See pillar tag — currently `practice`, but if recruitment becomes part of the project, `philosophy` may also belong.

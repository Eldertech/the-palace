What changed from Stage 1

Stage 1 was a still pond — nine octaves of one pitch class fused into a single drone. Stage 2 lets the pitch class step up. Each press of Step Up shifts every voice by one semitone. After twelve steps the stack has climbed an octave, and yet the perceived range never leaves the window. Hold the button or turn on Auto-Step and the staircase climbs forever.

The wrap is on purpose. When the top voice reaches the ceiling it drops nine octaves and re-enters at the bottom — and we flash that moment in red so you can see the seam the illusion is meant to hide. That is the lesson of Stage 2: the trick has a join, and you can hear how invisible it is when the window does its job.

What to listen for

Press Step Up twelve times slowly. The first press should sound like the whole drone shifted up a semitone. The twelfth should arrive on a pitch class one octave above where you started — and the drone should sound roughly identical to where it began, even though one voice has just wrapped from C9 down to C0. If the wrap is audible — a click, a phase pop, a sudden hole in the spectrum — that is the seam the Shepard window is meant to bury. Stage 2's job is to let you hear how well that burial works under your ear, not under a smoothed render.

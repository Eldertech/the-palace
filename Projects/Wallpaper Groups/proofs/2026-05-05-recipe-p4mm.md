# Recipe — Cooking p4mm into an 8-bar drum phrase

**The group:** p4mm. A square lattice with 4-fold rotation centers, mirror lines along both diagonals and both axes. Eight symmetry operations. The most "policed" of the simple groups.

**The fundamental domain:** an eighth of a measure. Two beats — and within those, an eighth of the voice-grid. Anything you place inside it will be reflected and rotated to fill the bar.

## Ingredients

- One bar of 4/4 at 120 BPM, divided into 16 sixteenth-notes
- A grid: rows = drum voices (kick, snare, hat-closed, hat-open, perc), columns = sixteenth-note positions
- Mirror axes: vertical at position 8 (mid-bar), horizontal between snare-and-kick rows
- Rotation centers: corners and bar-midpoint

## Method

1. **Place 3–4 hits in the fundamental domain only** — the first quarter of the bar, top half of the voice-grid. Don't think about the rest of the bar. Don't think about how it loops.
2. **Reflect across the vertical axis.** Whatever you placed in beats 1–2, mirror it onto beats 3–4. The bar is now symmetrical around the mid-point.
3. **Rotate 90° at the bar-midpoint.** Voices swap roles by their grid position: what was kick becomes hat, hat becomes snare, etc., placed at rotated time-positions.
4. **Reflect again** across both diagonals to enforce the full 8-element symmetry of p4mm.
5. The bar is now full and symmetric. Loop it for 8 bars.

## What you should hear

The phrase is *too symmetrical to be groove.* It will feel like a snowflake more than a beat. That is the point. p4mm has too much symmetry to swing — every accent has a mirror, every push has a pull, every kick has a snare-shaped echo at the rotated position.

Now break it: replace one hit in beats 9–16 with silence, or shift one hit by a sixteenth. The asymmetry will *blaze*. p4mm broken in one place is more compelling than p1 freestyled.

## What this teaches

You don't compose *with* a wallpaper group. You compose *against* it. The group is the floor; what you remove from the floor is the music. *Symmetry is the wall the rhythm leans on, never the rhythm itself.*

Try p3 next (only 3-fold rotation, no reflection): the breaks land differently because the floor is differently asymmetric. Each of the 17 groups has its own break-vocabulary. *That* is the library.

# BLUELINE M3.6 — the delta sweep (report)

**Date:** 2026-06-19 · Mac-side Claude Code (feature/blueline-m3 worktree) · follows [m3.5-report.md](m3.5-report.md).
**Question:** M3.5 showed the (now-correct, white) flow-warped noise *ties* seed-lock at the extreme 483 px
jump. The bet ([[The Flow Field is the Spine]]) should pay off at **small** deltas — so does warped beat
seed-lock anywhere, and where is the crossover?

## The test

One pod, 11 renders, same prompt/params. The A→B pose interpolated at t ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.6, 1.0} →
flow-field deltas **48 / 96 / 169 / 290 / 483 px**. At each delta: **B-seed-lock** (`N_A` reused) vs
**B-warped** (`N_A` GtF-warped by the t-scaled flow), both scored against the anchor A. The warped noise is
white at every delta (lag-1 autocorr ≤ 0.002) and warp-filled rises 66 %→97 % as the delta shrinks — at 48 px
only 3 % of the field is fresh, so the warp is almost pure transport (its best case). `m3.6_sweep_prep.py` /
`m3.6_pod_render.py` / `m3.6_score.py`; curve in `renders-sweep/m3.6-sweep.png`.

## Result — NO crossover: warped and seed-lock are a tie ❌

| delta | seed-lock `embed`/`color` | warped `embed`/`color` | Δembed | Δcolor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48 px  | 0.957 / 0.818 | 0.922 / 0.779 | −0.035 | −0.039 |
| 96 px  | 0.918 / 0.651 | **0.941 / 0.879** | +0.023 | +0.228 |
| 169 px | 0.916 / 0.890 | 0.888 / 0.311 | −0.028 | −0.578 |
| 290 px | 0.707 / 0.459 | 0.708 / 0.077 | +0.001 | −0.382 |
| 483 px | 0.744 / 0.380 | 0.740 / 0.330 | −0.004 | −0.049 |

**On `embed_cos` (the pose-invariant identity/scene metric — the primary one) the two are indistinguishable
at every delta**: Δ oscillates within ±0.035 with no trend (mean −0.009), and the curves sit on top of each
other. The auto-detected "crossover ~290 px" is an artifact — the two embed lines merely graze (Δ = +0.001)
there. **On `color_corr` the warp is noisier, not better**: one clear win at 96 px (+0.228, and that frame
really does reproduce A — hooded figure, mist, light beam) but big losses at 169 px (−0.578) and 290 px
(−0.382); mean Δcolor −0.16. **No delta where flow-warped noise reliably beats seed-lock.**

So the predicted "warp wins at small delta" crossover **does not appear**. Two compounding reasons:

1. **Seed-lock is already near-ceiling at small deltas** (embed 0.92–0.96 at ≤169 px). The pose ControlNet
   is doing the heavy lifting of placing content; reusing the shared latent anchors identity well on its own.
   There is almost no headroom for the warp to improve on, so it can only match or add noise.
2. **The test is variance-dominated.** One sample per cell, FLUX run-to-run variance, linearly-interpolated
   (slightly unnatural) intermediate poses, and a whole-image embedding sensitive to background — the 96 px
   win and the 169 px color crash are the signature of noise, not signal. A real effect this subtle (if it
   exists) needs replication (N seeds per delta, error bars) to surface.

## What M3 → M3.6 together establish

- **M3:** the inject path works; the *naive* warp collapses to rainbow (a whiteness bug).
- **M3.5:** the *correct* warp (forward-splat + L2-norm + hole-fill) is coherent and white; at 483 px it ties.
- **M3.6:** swept 48→483 px on a single pair, the correct warp **ties seed-lock on identity at every delta**
  and is noisier on palette. **For a single staged A→B jump of any size, flow-warped noise does not beat
  seed-lock.**

## The honest conclusion for the bet

[[The Flow Field is the Spine]]'s "move the noise at the render" is **not justified for single staged jumps**
— seed-lock is as good and simpler. Its *only* remaining viable regime is the one this design can't test:
a **cumulative multi-frame sequence**, where seed-lock's drift compounds frame-to-frame and a per-step
flow-warp re-aligns each increment (Go-with-the-Flow's actual video setting, and BLUELINE's true
along-the-clock motion). That is **M3.7** — a short rendered *sequence* (not a single jump), seed-lock vs
per-frame-warped, measuring drift accumulation, ideally with a couple of seeds for error bars.

**Design rule for BLUELINE now:** the storyboard→render path is **discrete panels with often-large jumps** →
use **seed-lock + identity + depth + img2img** for cross-panel coherence; **do not invest in flow-warped
noise there** — it adds cost and variance without a measured gain. Reserve noise-warping for *within-shot
motion sequences*, and only adopt it if M3.7's cumulative-drift test shows a real advantage. This is a
de-risking result: it tells BLUELINE where **not** to spend.

The bet survives only as a *within-shot cumulative-sequence* hypothesis → **M3.7 = the rendered-sequence /

identity + depth + img2img.

# BLUELINE M3.7 — the cumulative-sequence test (report)

**Date:** 2026-06-19 · Mac-side Claude Code (feature/blueline-m3 worktree) · follows [m3.6-report.md](m3.6-report.md).
**Question:** M3.6 showed the (correct, white) warp ties seed-lock on a single jump. Its **last viable regime**
is a multi-frame *sequence*: does a **per-step warped chain** (each frame's noise = the previous frame's noise
transported one increment — Go-with-the-Flow's actual video mechanism) read as **smoother motion** than
fixed-noise seed-lock, where drift compounds?

## The test

A 6-frame coil→leap motion (t = 0, .2, .4, .6, .8, 1.0), rendered two ways on one pod, same prompt:

- **seed-lock** — every frame uses the same `N_A`; only the pose moves.
- **warped chain** — `N_0 = N_A`; `N_i = GtF-warp(N_{i-1}, flow/5)`. The noise is transported step-by-step
  along the motion (white at every step, lag-1 ≈ 0.000, 93 % transported per increment).

Metric: **adjacent-frame consistency** (mean `embed_cos`/`color_corr` between consecutive frames) — the
temporal-smoothness signal a fixed noise supposedly can't give and a warped chain supposedly can. Filmstrip +
per-condition GIF for the eyeball. `m3.7_seq_prep.py` / `m3.7_pod_render.py` / `m3.7_score.py`.

## Result — the warped chain is NOT smoother; seed-lock wins ❌

| condition | adjacent `embed_cos` | adjacent `color_corr` | per-pair embed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **seed-lock** | **0.858** | **+0.596** | 0.918, 0.877, 0.786, 0.858, 0.850 |
| warped chain | 0.809 | +0.476 | 0.890, 0.882, 0.756, 0.721, 0.796 |
| Δ (warped − seedlock) | **−0.049** | **−0.119** | — |

Seed-lock produces **smoother** frame-to-frame transitions than the warped chain on both metrics, and the
filmstrip (`renders-seq/m3.7-filmstrip.png`, GIFs `seedlock.gif` / `warped.gif`) shows why: the seed-lock row
holds one hooded figure cleanly through the arm-raise, while the warped row **wanders** (a divergent
armored-looking figure appears at s3). The reason is mechanical: each warp step injects ~7 % fresh noise and
reshuffles the field, so consecutive *warped* frames share slightly *less* structure than consecutive
*seed-lock* frames (which share an identical latent). For per-frame text-to-image with a pose ControlNet
already placing the content, the noise's job is to stay **stable**, and fixed noise maximizes that —
"following the motion" actively hurts.

## The decisive conclusion for the bet

Across **every** regime now tested — single jump swept 48→483 px (M3.6) **and** a cumulative 6-frame
sequence (M3.7) — **flow-warped noise never beats seed-lock at the render.** Correctly implemented (M3.5:
white, coherent), it simply confers no measured benefit over the dead-simple baseline for per-frame T2I.
[[The Flow Field is the Spine]]'s "**move the noise at the render**" sub-claim is **retired**.

What survives — and it's most of the entry's value — is the flow field as the **compositional / FX spine**:
the M2 motion comic *scrolls the field along its direction*, the staging vocabulary rides it, the field
organizes layout and limited animation. That is real and verified. The one thing it does **not** do is warp
the diffusion init noise to buy render coherence — seed-lock + the pose ControlNet already own that.

(Caveat held honestly: this is per-frame FLUX T2I, not a video-diffusion model *trained* on warped noise —
the setting GtF was built for. The finding is "don't warp noise for BLUELINE's per-frame render," not
"GtF is wrong in its own domain.")

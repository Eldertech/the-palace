BLUELINE — M1 to M4

BLUELINE is a music-synced motion comic — inked panels that move on the song's clock. The build runs as a milestone ladder (M0 previz → M1 animatic → M2 motion comic → M3 flow-field FX → M4 hyperreal impact → M5 sync server). This is an honest account of M1–M4: what each one asked, what got built, and what the numbers actually said — including the places the answer was no.

Two of these milestones returned negatives. Those are not failures — a clean negative tells you where not to spend, which is half of what a proving ground is for.

M1

Animatic · the comic register Verified

Raise the storyboard to an inked comic — and grow a staging vocabulary

Can a storyboard carry enough authored structure to drive an AI render and a 3D transfer at the same time?

M1 took the rough previz boards and lifted them to the comic register: inked panels drawn over a blue-line draft (the project's namesake). The real product wasn't the prettier panels — it was the staging vocabulary that came with them: facing, eyeline, left/right laterality, and a framing-robust shoulder–shoulder–pelvis torso frame.

That vocabulary turned out to do three jobs with one description: it's the board-record schema (how a shot is stored), it's the AI's conditioning keypoints (how the render is posed), and it's a lossless 2D→3D transfer format (how a panel becomes a camera in space). M1 also re-founded the project on an established animation pipeline — anime backbone, comics skin, animated-feature tissue, music-video clock — rather than an invented one. The principle: adopt the craft, author the seam.

Took: one description that serves storage, rendering, and 3D at once is the spine the rest of the ladder hangs on. Verified and load-bearing.

M2

Motion comic · the panels move Verified

Make the held panels move — without becoming a video

Can the comic move on the music while staying an instrument (scrubbable, deterministic) rather than a baked clip?

M2 set the inked panels in motion as limited animation — staged, not simulated. The flow field scrolls along its direction (the first place the "flow field is the spine" idea actually moves), the figure breathes (feet anchored) and sways, and the ground parallaxes. No physics, no simulated contact — just authored motion layered on the held pose.

The bet that keeps it an instrument: every motion is a pure function of the song playhead — a continuous frame number, interpolated between beats and re-snapped on each beat. Verified the hard way: drawFrame(F) is pixel-identical for the same F and differs for different F. A motion-OFF toggle freezes back to exactly the M1 held register, and the staging keypoints stay lossless (the shimmer is render-only).

Took: motion as a pure function of the playhead means you can scrub, loop, and sync to a live tempo without re-rendering. It's an instrument, not a movie file.

M3

Flow-field FX at the render Honest negative

Can the flow field reach the actual diffusion render?

If you warp the render's starting noise along the motion, does the look hold together better than just reusing the same noise?

This is the milestone I worked through this session, and it has four rungs because the first answer was wrong for an interesting reason. The contest throughout: flow-warped noise (move the init noise along the flow, per Go-with-the-Flow) versus the dead-simple baseline, seed-lock (just reuse the same noise for both frames). Two yardsticks:

embedding cosine — how similar two renders are as scene / identity (pose-invariant; the primary metric).  ·  colour correlation — how well the palette / costume matches.

M3 · the inject path works — the naive warp does not honest negative

Built the plumbing to feed an external noise tensor into the render deterministically. Tested at a big 482 px pose jump. Seed-lock degraded gracefully (0.744 / 0.380). The naive warp — a backward image-warp plus a global renormalize — collapsed into rainbow garbage (0.508 / 0.016). The reason is precise: fixing the mean/variance doesn't fix spatial whiteness, and the diffusion model renders correlated noise as psychedelic striping.

M3.5 · fix the warp — now it's coherent, but only a tie fixed → level

Implemented the warp the correct way (the HIWYN / Go-with-the-Flow core: forward-splat the noise along the flow, normalize each cell by the L2 norm of its weights, fill disocclusion holes with fresh noise). The warped noise is now as statistically white as the base noise (spatial autocorrelation 0.001 vs the naive 0.127). The rainbow is gone — the warped frame is a real, palette-matched figure (0.709 / 0.373) — but at 482 px it lands level with seed-lock, not beating it.

M3.6 · sweep the delta — no crossover anywhere no win at any size

Maybe the warp wins at small jumps? Swept the pose delta across 48 / 96 / 169 / 290 / 483 px. On the primary metric the two are tied at every delta (difference within ±0.035, no trend — the lines sit on top of each other). Why: seed-lock is already near-ceiling (0.92–0.96) at small deltas, so there's no headroom to beat, and single renders carry too much run-to-run variance to resolve a subtle effect.

M3.7 · the last hope: a moving sequence — seed-lock still wins bet closed

The warp's real home is supposed to be sequences, where errors compound frame-to-frame. Rendered a 6-frame coil→leap motion two ways and measured adjacent-frame smoothness: fixed-noise seed-lock vs a per-step warped chain (each frame's noise is the previous frame's, transported one step). Seed-lock was smoother (adjacent 0.858 vs 0.809). The chain wanders — each warp step injects a little fresh noise, so consecutive warped frames share less structure than two identical-noise frames do.

Verdict on M3: across every regime tested — one big jump, a swept range, and a moving sequence — warping the noise never beats seed-lock at the render. The "move the noise at the render" idea is retired. The flow field keeps its real, verified value as the compositional / FX spine (M2's scrolling field and the staging vocabulary) — it just isn't a render-noise trick. The practical rule, now load-bearing: panel-render coherence rides seed-lock + pose ControlNet + identity + depth + img2img. The bet was disproven cheaply (~$0.50), which is exactly the job.

M4

Hyperreal impact · reconnaissance Green, gated

Does identity survive the comic → hyperreal style jump?

When a panel blows up into a cinematic "money shot", is it still recognizably the same character and scene?

M4 is the impact moment: a comic panel expanding into a photoreal render. The risk is identity across the style jump. As a reconnaissance spike, I rendered two boards in both registers — same pose, same noise, only the style prompt changes (comic ink vs hyperreal cinematic) — and measured how much the two versions still read as the same scene.

boardcomic ↔ hyper · embedcolour
A (coil)0.689−0.006
B (leap)0.752+0.018
mean0.721~0.00

Mean identity hold 0.72 — above Track II's 0.60 target, and that's with no identity model at all. (Colour correlation near zero is correct: the palette is meant to change between flat ink and cinematic grade.) So the "same face in two registers" risk is tractable — a dedicated identity model has clear headroom above this baseline.

Two honest caveats: the embedding cosine measures scene similarity (helped by the shared pose+noise), not rigorous face identity — that needs a face-ID test. And the comic register here is soft: from a prompt alone the model gives a stylized look, not crisp flat ink. Both gaps are exactly what Track II's style LoRA + PuLID are for.

Verdict on M4: green at the spike — identity/scene clears the bar across the style jump before any identity model is even added. Full M4 (crisp comic ink + rigorous face-hold) is gated on Track II, not on any new unknown. The blocker became a build.

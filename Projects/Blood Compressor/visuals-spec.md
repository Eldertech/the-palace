# Blood Compressor — Visuals Specification

The "weird visuals" dispatch brief for the [Blood Compressor radio-play lesson](radio-play-script.md). One visual per row (VIS01 through VIS10), each prepared as a brief the Maker can hand to the right Specialist.

This is a **spec sheet, not a dispatch order**. No Specialist is invoked here. The intent is that this document, in Maker's hands, is enough to write Job Contracts for p5.js, ComfyUI, and Manim CE in parallel.

## Visual Vocabulary (palette and timbre)

The lesson's mythology is *paramedical, not surgical*. Loudon's home-entry direction: "Not warm-and-tube, not surgical-and-clean. Embodied. You are looking inside someone's neck."

The visual palette is therefore:

- **Background**: deep near-black, `#0B0B10` (the same dark background as the [[Kuramoto Coupling]] palette per the Maker's Recipe entry — house consistency)
- **Body red**: `#C8254C` (oxygenated arterial blood, slightly desaturated from pure red — this is the *home* color)
- **Vessel wall**: `#8B4A5E` (a muted plum-rose; the color of artery muscle layer when viewed in cross-section)
- **Baroreceptor glow**: `#F5C16C` (warm amber, distinct from the indigo/amber Kuramoto palette but in the same family)
- **Pressure curve / data overlay**: `#E8E8F0` (cool off-white for graph lines, type, technical overlays)
- **Pulse highlight**: pure white `#FFFFFF` reserved for the *moment of beat* — the heartbeat flash. Used sparingly.

**No saturated greens, no clinical blues, no metallic silver/chrome.** Those colors break the embodied frame. If a Specialist proposes a metallic surface in a workflow, flag and reroute.

## Aspect & Frame Rate

Per [[Shop/Maker]] house standards: **1920×1080**, **30 fps** for video, **60 fps** for the interactive p5.js demos. All visuals deliver at 1080p; the Loudon Live publish may upscale, but the source renders at 1080.

## Visual Inventory

### VIS01 — beating-heart-loop

- **Where**: Cold open, 0:00–1:30
- **Specialist**: **p5.js** (looping web animation)
- **Type**: 60 bpm beating-heart silhouette, single-color
- **Brief**: A single anatomical heart silhouette, drawn as a vector path, in `#C8254C` against `#0B0B10`. Beats at 60 bpm via a scale transform synced to the sine of (time × 1 Hz). At each beat moment, a brief white flash (`#FFFFFF` for 50ms) ripples outward from the heart's center as a single-frame highlight. Loop is seamless. No anatomical detail visible — this is a *silhouette*, not a diagram. Filename: `vis01-beating-heart-loop.html` (and exported PNG sequence for the radio-play video track).
- **Tier**: Sketch (very simple sketch; p5.js's native strength)
- **Notes**: The heart's *shape* should be slightly stylized — the iconic two-chambered cartoon heart silhouette would be wrong here. Use an anatomically-leaning silhouette: distinct ventricles, an aorta arching off the top, slightly asymmetric. ComfyUI could generate the reference silhouette if p5.js authoring from scratch is too slow; if so, ComfyUI produces a single reference image and p5.js animates the scaling.

### VIS02 — title-card

- **Where**: 1:30–1:34 (4s)
- **Specialist**: **ComfyUI** (single still image)
- **Type**: Title card
- **Brief**: Title text "BLOOD COMPRESSOR" in a large serif (system serif per house standard), subtitle "*a 30-minute Loudon Live lesson*" in italic, color `#E8E8F0` on `#0B0B10`. The text sits over a faint, dark background image generated by ComfyUI: an extremely dark, low-contrast cross-section of vascular tissue, almost subliminal — the viewer should *barely* see it. The text is the foreground; the tissue is a texture suggestion.
- **Prompt for ComfyUI**: *"cross-section of human arterial tissue, microscopic view, extremely dark and low contrast, faint suggestion of vessel walls and blood cells, near-black background, deep red and plum-rose tones, no clinical lighting, suitable as subliminal texture under text, painterly rather than photographic"*
- **Workflow**: Flux.1 Schnell, 1024×576 (will be upscaled to 1080p at composite time), seed pinned for reproducibility, no LoRA.
- **Tier**: Sketch
- **Notes**: Output is a single PNG. Maker composites the text overlay via p5.js or a simple HTML/CSS layout — ComfyUI does not render text well.

### VIS03 — threshold-line

- **Where**: Scene 1, 1:30–6:00 (background during scene)
- **Specialist**: **Manim CE** (precise math motion)
- **Type**: A single horizontal line being drawn slowly across the screen, labeled "threshold" — this is the moment the lesson's central concept enters the screen as an abstraction
- **Brief**: A clean black background. A single horizontal line, color `#E8E8F0`, animates from left to right over 4 seconds (so it's complete within the first 4s of Scene 1's voiceover). Label "threshold" in serif text appears below the line at completion. The line then *holds* for the rest of Scene 1 while the voiceover plays — it is the screen's only graphic element, doing pedagogical work by presence.
- **Manim scene**: simple `Line` mobject with `Create()` animation, `Text("threshold")` underneath, no further motion. ~30 lines of Python.
- **Tier**: Sketch
- **Notes**: This is the *most restrained* visual in the lesson. Resist the temptation to add ornament. The line is the threshold, and the listener should be reminded of it without distraction.

### VIS04 — neck-cross-section

- **Where**: Scene 1, ~3:00–6:00 transitions into Scene 2
- **Specialist**: **ComfyUI** (still image; possibly with subtle motion overlay via p5.js)
- **Type**: Anatomically-accurate stylized cross-section of a human neck, showing carotid artery in profile, blood visibly moving inside
- **Brief**: A stylized but anatomically-correct cross-section of the human neck. Skin layer at the surface, sternocleidomastoid muscle, then the carotid artery in cross-section. The artery's interior is visible — a circular cross-section showing the arterial wall (muscle layer, intima, endothelium) and the blood inside (`#C8254C`). The image is *painterly*, not medical-textbook. Slightly oversaturated reds. No labels at this stage (labels come in VIS05 via overlay).
- **Prompt for ComfyUI**: *"painterly cross-section of human neck, anatomically accurate stylized illustration, carotid artery visible in profile, dark plum and arterial red palette, dark near-black background, single light source from upper left, no labels or text, oversaturated reds for arterial blood, slightly stylized line work suggesting medical illustration but warmer and more embodied than clinical, palette: #C8254C, #8B4A5E, #0B0B10"*
- **Workflow**: Flux.1 Dev (higher fidelity than Schnell for anatomy), 1920×1080, palette-locked via IP-Adapter with a 3-image palette reference (the Maker's house palette swatch), seed pinned.
- **Motion overlay**: p5.js takes the rendered still and overlays a moving "pressure" line traveling through the carotid lumen — a small sinusoidal trace. This is the second layer of the visual; ComfyUI produces the substrate, p5.js produces the motion.
- **Tier**: Study (this is the lesson's signature image; needs to be *good*)
- **Notes**: Strong candidate for **Comparison Mode** between ComfyUI and Midjourney. ComfyUI gives palette control; Midjourney gives aesthetic ceiling. Recommendation: render in both, pick the one that lands the "painterly not clinical" instruction better. If only one render, choose ComfyUI for palette discipline.

### VIS05 — baroreceptors-firing

- **Where**: Scene 2, 6:00–10:30 (interactive sketch)
- **Specialist**: **p5.js** (interactive sketch with parameter slider)
- **Type**: An interactive visualization of pressure rising along a vessel, crossing a threshold band, and triggering baroreceptor firing + vessel constriction
- **Brief**:
  - **Static layer**: a horizontal vessel runs across the screen (a long pill shape, `#8B4A5E` wall, `#C8254C` interior). Baroreceptors appear as small `#F5C16C` dots embedded in the inner wall surface — 8 of them spaced along the vessel.
  - **Dynamic layer**: a pressure trace (a sinusoidal wave with random small variations) moves left-to-right along the inside of the vessel. The trace's amplitude is the pressure level.
  - **Threshold band**: a horizontal `#E8E8F0` band crosses the screen at a configurable Y level. The trace must pass through this band to "exceed threshold."
  - **Interactivity**: a slider (HTML range input bound to p5.js sketch) lets the viewer drag the threshold line up or down. As the trace exceeds the threshold, the baroreceptors *light up* (color shift from `#F5C16C` to `#FFFFFF` for 150ms, then fade back) and the vessel wall *constricts inward* — visibly narrows by ~40% — and the pressure inside drops.
  - **Reset**: the cycle loops. The pressure rises, crosses, fires receptors, vessel narrows, pressure drops, vessel re-expands. ~6 second cycle, then loop.
- **Deployment**: single HTML file, 1920×1080 canvas, embeddable in the Loudon Live page.
- **Tier**: Study (interactive; needs polish)
- **Notes**: This is the visual *centerpiece* of Scene 2. It encodes the lesson's central insight (threshold-as-zone, knee-as-gradient) into a thing the viewer can manipulate. Resist adding features (don't add ratio, attack, release sliders here — those come in VIS09). One concept, one slider.

### VIS06 — vessel-wall-muscle-closeup + ratio-curves-overlay

- **Where**: Scene 3, 10:30–15:00
- **Specialist**: **ComfyUI** (close-up still) + **Manim CE** (animated ratio curves overlay)
- **Type**: Compound visual — ComfyUI produces an extreme close-up of the artery wall's smooth muscle layer; Manim CE produces the math-graph overlay
- **Brief part A (ComfyUI)**: Extreme close-up of an artery's smooth muscle layer. Concentric rings of muscle fiber wrapped around the vessel. Visible cellular detail but stylized — painterly, not microscope-photographic. As the scene's audio progresses, the muscle fibers visibly contract (this contraction is added at composite time by warping the still image with p5.js, *not* by ComfyUI generating a video).
  - **Prompt**: *"extreme close-up of human arterial smooth muscle layer, concentric rings of muscle fiber wrapped around a vessel, painterly anatomical illustration, deep arterial red and plum-rose, near-black background, slightly stylized cellular detail, single dramatic light from upper left, palette: #C8254C, #8B4A5E, #0B0B10, dramatic and embodied not clinical"*
  - **Workflow**: Flux.1 Dev, 1920×1080, IP-Adapter palette lock, seed pinned.
- **Brief part B (Manim CE)**: Animated overlay showing input-vs-output curves at ratios 1:1, 2:1, 4:1, 10:1, and ∞:1. Each curve in a distinct color (use a palette derived from the body-red base — variations of saturation and brightness on `#C8254C`). Curves animate in one at a time, synced to NARRATOR's voiceover as each ratio is named.
  - **Manim scene**: `axes = Axes(...)`; create five `FunctionGraph` mobjects for the five ratios; `Create` them sequentially with `LaggedStart` synced to voiceover timings (provided by Maker from Whisper's word-timing extraction of the NARRATOR track).
  - **Output**: a transparent-background MP4 at 1920×1080, 30 fps, ~30s duration. Composited over the ComfyUI close-up at mix time via ffmpeg.
- **Tier**: Study (the math overlay must be precise)
- **Notes**: This is a *compound visual* — the rare case where Manim and ComfyUI both contribute to the same shot. The Maker writes one Job Contract per Specialist, but the *segment plan* (per the Maker's mixed-motion heuristic) names the overlay timing explicitly so neither Specialist has to know about the other's deliverable.

### VIS07 — action-potential-and-attack-delay

- **Where**: Scene 4, 15:00–20:00 (with interactive moment)
- **Specialist**: **Manim CE** (the action potential animation) + **p5.js** (the interactive attack-time slider demo)
- **Type**: Compound — a Manim CE animation of an action potential traveling along a neuron, paired with a p5.js interactive showing how attack time changes the gap between threshold-crossing and vessel-response
- **Brief part A (Manim CE)**: A neuron diagram running left-to-right across the screen — soma on the left, long axon, terminal on the right. A single action potential — visualized as a `#F5C16C` glowing pulse — travels along the axon from soma to terminal over ~120ms. As it reaches the terminal, a synaptic gap is briefly shown, then a second "signal" continues onward to the vessel wall (off-screen right). The visualization is *stylized*, not literal — the axon is a stylized line, the neuron a stylized blob, the pulse a glowing dot. Time axis can be shown beneath if it helps; tested without first.
  - **Manim scene**: ~50 lines. Use `MovingArrow` or a custom mobject for the traveling pulse. Speed of travel is real-time (120ms) for the animation, but for the *radio play* the cue is played at ~3× speed-down so the listener can perceive the delay — flagged in the segment plan.
  - **Output**: transparent-background MP4, 1920×1080.
- **Brief part B (p5.js)**: An interactive showing a pressure trace, threshold line, and vessel response. A slider controls **attack time** from 1ms to 200ms. As the slider moves, the gap between threshold-crossing and the visible onset of vessel constriction visibly changes. At 1ms: the vessel slams shut at the threshold-crossing moment (no breath). At 200ms: the pressure visibly overshoots the threshold before the vessel responds.
  - **Deployment**: single HTML, same canvas dimensions as VIS05.
- **Tier**: Study (both)
- **Notes**: VIS07 is the *most pedagogically dense* visual in the lesson. The interactive (part B) is what students will return to outside the lesson. Make it standalone-shareable.

### VIS08 — muscle-relaxation + release-curves

- **Where**: Scene 5, 20:00–24:30
- **Specialist**: **p5.js** (the relaxation animation) + **Manim CE** (the release-curve graph overlay)
- **Type**: Compound — a slow vessel-relaxation animation paired with an overlay graph showing release curves (linear, exponential, logarithmic, program-dependent)
- **Brief part A (p5.js)**: An animation of an artery, having constricted, slowly opening back up over 6 seconds. The *shape of the opening curve* is the teaching: not a mirror of the closing, but a slow exhale. The opening is visibly slower than the closing (the closing happens over ~500ms; the opening over 6 seconds). Color and palette per house standards.
- **Brief part B (Manim CE)**: An animated graph showing four release curves — linear, exponential, logarithmic, program-dependent. The graphs animate in one at a time, synced to NARRATOR's voiceover. Each curve is in a distinct shade of the body-red palette. Axes labeled "time" and "gain return." The program-dependent curve is *visibly different* from the others — it changes shape based on how long the compression was engaged.
  - **Output**: transparent-background MP4, composited via ffmpeg.
- **Tier**: Study
- **Notes**: VIS08's central teaching is the *asymmetry of attack and release*. The p5.js animation (part A) is the felt embodiment; the Manim CE graphs (part B) are the formalization. Together they let the lesson land in both the body and the brain.

### VIS09 — full-instrument

- **Where**: Scene 6, 24:30–29:00 (the centerpiece interactive)
- **Specialist**: **p5.js** (the most ambitious interactive in the lesson)
- **Type**: The full Blood Compressor instrument as the home entry envisions it — vessel running across the screen, beating heart at the bottom, baroreceptors on the vessel wall, all four parameters labeled, particles flowing through, vessel constricting in response to audio
- **Brief**:
  - **Static layout**:
    - Center: horizontal vessel running across the screen (long pill, scaled larger than VIS05).
    - Bottom-center: beating heart silhouette (the same shape as VIS01) synced to a slowly-varying "RMS" signal — actually a deterministic curve baked into the sketch, not an audio input.
    - 8 baroreceptors on the vessel wall (more visible than VIS05; the centerpiece deserves slightly larger dots).
    - 4 parameter labels positioned around the vessel: *threshold* (a horizontal line crossing the vessel), *ratio* (a vessel-elasticity indicator on the wall), *attack* (a labeled span on the timeline), *release* (a second labeled span on the timeline).
  - **Animation**:
    - A pressure trace (matching the SFX14 "compressed vocal" cue's envelope, baked into the sketch) moves left-to-right inside the vessel.
    - At each beat of the heart, the pressure increases.
    - When the pressure crosses the threshold line, the baroreceptors light up, and the vessel wall *constricts* over the attack time (visibly delayed from the crossing).
    - The pressure drops; the wall *opens* over the release time (visibly slower than the attack).
    - Red blood particles flow along the vessel interior — when the vessel narrows, the particles bunch up; when it opens, they flow freely. This is the "embodied physics" of the lesson.
  - **Interactivity (deferred)**: The first version of VIS09 is *non-interactive* — it plays out the demo for the radio play's Scene 6. A later iteration could expose sliders for all four parameters and become the interactive build-along device for the Max for Live lesson.
- **Deployment**: single HTML, 1920×1080, embeddable; also a 30-second screen capture as MP4 for the radio play's video track.
- **Tier**: **Piece** (this is the lesson's signature interactive; the home entry's Open Question #1 about the heart-pulse audio depends on this visual landing correctly)
- **Notes**: VIS09 is the closest thing to a *Piece-tier* deliverable in the lesson. It is the visual proof that the home entry's interface mythology is sound. Render at high polish; expect 1–2 hours of authoring time.

### VIS10 — closing-card

- **Where**: Outro, 29:00–30:00
- **Specialist**: **ComfyUI** (with text overlay composited)
- **Type**: Closing title card
- **Brief**: Same visual language as VIS02 (title card). Text: "BLOOD COMPRESSOR — A Loudon Live lesson. Build it next." in serif, color `#E8E8F0` on `#0B0B10`. Background: a *brighter* version of the VIS02 vascular tissue texture — the lesson has illuminated the body, so the closing image is less dark than the opening. Small footer text reads "Loudon Live · Autodidact Polymaths" per [[Loudon Live]] convention.
- **Prompt for ComfyUI**: Same as VIS02 but with the prompt addition "*slightly brighter overall, faint warm glow suggesting that what was hidden has been illuminated*"
- **Tier**: Sketch
- **Notes**: Output is a single PNG. Maker composites the text overlay.

## Resource Scheduling Notes (Maker discipline)

Per [[Shop/Maker]] § Resource Scheduling:

- **Cannot run in parallel**: Two ComfyUI jobs (VRAM contention). VIS02, VIS04, VIS06A, VIS10 must run sequentially. Estimated ~10 min wall-clock each at Study tier → 40 minutes serial.
- **Cannot run in parallel**: Manim CE Piece-tier render + Whisper transcription. The radio-play script will be sent through Whisper to extract word-timings for syncing Manim animations. Sequence: Whisper first (gets word-timings), then Manim renders use those timings. Whisper job is ~5 minutes wall-clock; Manim jobs are ~5 minutes wall-clock each → 20 minutes serial.
- **Can run in parallel**: p5.js sketches are CPU-bound and authoring-time-dominated, not render-bound. All 5 can be drafted in parallel and tested in browser. Each ~30 minutes authoring → 2.5 hours human-time, but no host contention.

**Total cycle wall-clock estimate**: ~3.5 hours from Maker dispatch to all visuals delivered (heavy on the ComfyUI sequencing). The p5.js sketches dominate the authoring time; the Manim and ComfyUI work is mostly waiting on renders.

## Comparison Mode Candidates

Per [[Shop/Maker]] § Comparison Mode, the briefs that genuinely benefit from Comparison Mode:

1. **VIS04 (neck cross-section)** — ComfyUI vs Midjourney. The "painterly not clinical" instruction is exactly the place where Midjourney's aesthetic ceiling might win.
2. **VIS09 (full instrument)** — could be rendered in both p5.js and Tone.js+HTML for a more polished audio-reactive version. Recommendation: only spawn this Comparison if VIS09 in p5.js lands soft.

Other briefs are routed unambiguously and don't need Comparison Mode.

## House-Style Deviations

- The **interactive sliders** on VIS05 and VIS07-B deviate from a pure passive radio-play format — these are the moments where the lesson *invites the listener to drag a thing*. The radio play is meant to be experienced as audio + video, but the centerpiece visuals are standalone-shareable interactives that survive outside the lesson.
- VIS09's deferred-interactivity (build-version is non-interactive; full-version is the Max for Live deliverable) is a deliberate two-stage release. Flag this in the Maker's job report so the Stage 2 Max build-along lesson can pick up where Scene 6 leaves off.

## What This Spec Does Not Yet Decide

- **Captions / accessibility**: the radio play needs captions for the spoken voice — that's a Whisper dispatch the Maker would generate from the Kokoro tracks, but the visual placement of captions in the final video composite is undecided.
- **Loudon-voice variant**: when the script is re-rendered with Loudon's voice in the NARRATOR slot (Piece tier), some of the visuals may need re-syncing to the new word timings. Whisper handles the re-sync; no visual re-render is needed.
- **Mobile / vertical format**: 1920×1080 is the source format. Whether to render a 9:16 vertical cut for social distribution is a Loudon Live cadence question, not a Blood Compressor question.

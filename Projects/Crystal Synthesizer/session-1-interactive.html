Crystal Synthesizer — Session 1

Crystals don't create color.
They reveal what's already there.

White light contains every frequency. A crystal's internal geometry — its lattice, its symmetry — determines which frequencies go where. The crystal is a sorter, not a painter.

Optical dispersion — cubic crystal

This is dispersion: the refractive index of a crystal depends on wavelength. Violet light slows down more than red light inside the crystal, so it bends more sharply on exit. The result is a spectrum — but the spectrum was always implicit in the white light. The crystal made it visible.

The same physics governs how the crystal vibrates. Its symmetry determines which vibrational modes — phonons — are allowed. Different crystals allow different modes. Different modes mean different frequencies. Different frequencies mean different sound.

If we moved from light to sound — if we built an instrument whose partials were determined by crystal physics — what would each crystal actually sound like?

We don't know yet. We have hypotheses. Today we build the instrument to find out.

From THz to Hz:
scaling crystal physics into audio

Real crystals vibrate at terahertz frequencies — trillions of oscillations per second, far above hearing. But the ratios between modes are what matter for timbre. We scale the whole structure down into the audio range while preserving the relationships.

Crystal ω(k) = 2√(K/m) · |sin(k·a/2)|
Phonon dispersion relation — maps wavevector k to angular frequency ω. K = spring constant, m = atomic mass, a = lattice spacing.
Scaling f_audio = f_phonon × (1 / scaling_factor)
We scale all mode frequencies by the same constant. Ratios are preserved. Timbre is preserved. Only the register changes.
Result partial set = { f₁, f₂, f₃ ... fₙ }
Each mode becomes a partial. Their amplitude ratios are determined by the excitation and mode density. Their frequency ratios are determined by the crystal.

The absolute frequencies are arbitrary — we choose the scaling factor to land in a musically useful register. What the crystal actually determines is the ratios between partials. That ratio structure is the timbre. That's what we're building.

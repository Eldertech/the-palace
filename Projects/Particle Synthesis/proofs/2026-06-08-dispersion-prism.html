Particle Synthesis — the dispersion prism

One wideband grain is not one particle — it is a cloud of partials, each a particle carrying its own wavenumber k. They all launch from the same point at the same instant. A dispersion relation ω(k) gives each partial a different velocity, so the cloud spreads as it flies: high partials race ahead, low ones lag. That spatial spreading is the spectral evolution you hear. Set dispersion to zero and every partial moves in lockstep — a static harmonic tone, "parameter modulation." Turn it up and the grain colours itself the way a bell or struck bar does, with no modulation path programmed in. The geometry is the synthesis.

In a non-dispersive medium ω(k) is a straight line: all partials share one velocity, nothing separates, the tone is static. A stiff bar bends that line upward — higher partials get sharper and faster, exactly the inharmonicity of a bell.

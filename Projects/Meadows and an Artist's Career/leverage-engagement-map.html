The diagnostic predicted this — and then overshot it. The project's central claim is that artists spend 80% of their attention at the bottom of the ladder and 1% at the top, and that the leverage runs the opposite direction. Handed the top tier on a plate, with my drafts already written in your voice, you still wrote nothing there. That is not avoidance. Three readings, and only you can say which is true:
  • The drafts are accurate. You read 4·3·2·1, recognized yourself, and had nothing to correct — the silence is agreement, and the paradigm rows are already true as written.
  • The instrument runs out. The worksheet — draft-a-row, correct-a-row — is a level-12-shaped tool. It works for parameters and ratios and rules. At the paradigm tier it stops fitting, the way a ruler stops measuring once you ask it about meaning. The top four want a different instrument: a walk, a dialogic page, a conversation.
  • You haven't been there yet. The reading of yourself at the paradigm level isn't written because it isn't finished — these are live, not settled, and writing them down would falsely fix something still in motion.
Whichever it is, the map is the teaching artifact the worksheet was built to produce: the place a person won't write is the place the real leverage is.

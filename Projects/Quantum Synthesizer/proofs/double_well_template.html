Quantum Synthesizer · study · double well

Raise a wall in the well, and every partial starts to beat

Start from the clean parabola. Push a bump up through its middle until it becomes two wells joined by a wall. A particle can only cross that wall by tunnelling, and tunnelling splits every energy level into a close pair. Two partials that close together beat. The pairs sitting higher see a thinner wall, so they tunnel faster and beat faster.

V(x) = ½ · x² + h · exp(−x² / 2σ²), σ = 0.5
(well height at x) = ½ × (position)² + (wall height) × (a bump of width σ)
fₙ = 196 Hz × (Eₙ − E₀ + 1)
(nth partial) = (G3) × (level n − ground + 1): each state rings at its own energy

The first strike is the bare parabola: twelve partials on the harmonic series, the same partials as the earlier clean tone. As the wall climbs, neighbouring levels slide together into pairs: the blue state (even, the same on both sides) and the gold state (odd, flipped across the wall) come to share one energy, almost. The last strike rings out over the finished double well so you can hear the pairs beat: slowly on G3, faster above it.

2 · drop a particle in the left well and listen

No additive recipe here. A wave packet is placed in the left well and the Schrödinger equation is run forward in time. The sound is the wavefunction read at one point inside the left well, the way a pickup reads one point on a string. The loudness at each moment is how much of the particle is at the pickup, so the tone fades as the particle tunnels to the right and swells as it comes back.

The packet mostly fills the lowest two pairs, so the slow 1.4 Hz swell (the particle crossing and returning about once every 0.7 s) carries a faster 6.3 Hz flutter from the next pair up. The trace is computed from the solver, not from the audio; that the loudness follows it is the claim the page is showing you.

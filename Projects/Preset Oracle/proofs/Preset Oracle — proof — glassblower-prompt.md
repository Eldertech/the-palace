# Oracle prompt — for the next student encounter

> ## A glassblower remembering an argument with their teacher
>
> The shop is closed. The furnace is at 1100°C. The molten gather on your blowpipe weighs 6 kg and you are alone. You are remembering, with your hands, an argument from twenty years ago — an argument with your teacher about whether glass remembers being a liquid.
>
> Make the sound that lives in the glass.
>
> The argument was about whether the structure of glass at room temperature is the structure of a frozen liquid or the structure of a true solid. Your teacher said one thing. You said the other. Neither of you was wrong.
>
> You have until the gather cools.

---

## What this prompt is doing — design notes for the oracle keeper

The prompt's pieces, and what each is trying to provoke in the student.

**The 1100°C furnace** — places the student in a *physical* state. The body is alert before the synthesizer is even touched. The student must work *hot* — there's an implied tempo. They cannot deliberate forever. This is a tempo constraint disguised as a temperature.

**The 6 kg gather** — weight. This is asking the synthesizer for *physicality* in the timbre. Not abstract glass-shimmer; molten weight. Big, slow, dense. A studio of clinking glass crystals would miss the point.

**The remembered argument** — a *cognitive* frame layered onto the physical. The student is doing two things at once. Memory in the body. This is asking the synthesizer to carry two voices: the present labor (the heat, the weight) and the absent argument (the teacher's voice, twenty years gone).

**"Whether glass remembers being a liquid"** — the philosophical core. This is the actual oracle question. Glass is a real thing whose state is famously contested in physics. The student is being asked to take a *position* on a real ambiguity, with their fingers, in sound. Two answers are both true; the synthesizer must commit to one.

**"Neither of you was wrong"** — the relief, the permission. Removes the trap of *having to be right*. The synthesizer's answer is allowed to be partial, contradictory, beautiful.

**"Until the gather cools"** — the closing tempo. There is an end. The piece is bounded. No infinite drone solos.

---

## What I expect the encounter to surface

**The good failures.** A student tries to be too literal. Reaches for a glass-tinkling preset and a slow LFO on a pad. Misses the heat. The oracle is doing its job by exposing this as a miss — the student must come back with something denser.

**The good successes.** A student spends 20 minutes adjusting a feedback path on a physical-modeled FM synth and finds a single timbre that is heavy enough to be molten, but with a high partial that *almost* sings — the teacher's voice, almost. They commit. They stop adjusting. They play one phrase. The oracle has produced an encounter.

**The unexpected.** A student doesn't make a *sound*. Makes silence with one hold-pedal-down in the middle of a performance. Says: "the gather is at the moment where it would either crack or stretch, and I don't know which." That's also a valid answer.

---

## Documentation hooks for the project

This prompt is calibrated to produce *quotable* student responses — answers that the project's forward vector requires for growth. Each encounter should capture:

- The synthesizer / preset(s) the student reached for
- The phrase they played (recordable)
- The philosophical answer they gave (transcribable)
- The thing that surprised them (the asymmetry between intent and result)

Three encounters with this prompt should produce enough material to understand whether it teaches as well as *Parmenides Dying Alone in a Warehouse* does.

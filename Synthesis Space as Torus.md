# Synthesis Space as Torus

A wavetable is not a rectangle. It is a torus.

This is not a metaphor. The topology of wavetable synthesis space is exactly **T² = S¹ × S¹** — the product of two circles. Every point on its surface encodes a complete synthesis state. Paths through it at constant angular velocities are torus knots. Adding inharmonicity as a third cyclic dimension produces **T³**, and the shapes generated by modulation become knotted tori and torus links in 3-space. The geometry is precise and it matters — it determines what synthesis operations are even possible, and it connects wavetable synthesis to a family of structures that appear across physics, mathematics, and music theory.

---

## The T² Structure of Wavetable Synthesis

A standard wavetable has two dimensions:

- **Phase** φ ∈ [0, 2π): position within one waveform cycle. The end connects back to the beginning — this dimension is a circle.
- **Frame position** θ ∈ [0, 2π): position within the wavetable's frame sequence. If the table is treated as circular (wrapping from last frame to first), this dimension is also a circle.

The product of two circles is a torus. The surface of that torus **is** the wavetable — every point (φ, θ) is a unique synthesis state, and the full wavetable has been traversed exactly when both circles have been completed.

Crucially: reading a waveform is not scanning a flat surface but travelling around one circle of the torus at a fixed θ. Morphing through frames is travelling around the other circle at a fixed φ. Doing both simultaneously — modulating wavetable position while playing a pitch — is tracing a diagonal path across the torus surface.

### Torus Knots on T²

When that diagonal path has a constant angular velocity in both dimensions, its character depends entirely on the ratio of the two rates:

- **Rational ratio p/q** (in lowest terms): the path closes after p revolutions of the phase circle and q revolutions of the frame circle. It is a **(p,q) torus knot** — a closed curve on the surface of a torus in 3-space. The trefoil is the (2,3) torus knot. The cinquefoil is (2,5).
- **Irrational ratio**: the path never closes. It covers the torus surface densely — passing arbitrarily close to every point. This is the **ergodic** case: an aperiodic, infinitely non-repeating traversal of the entire wavetable space.

A **sawtooth oscillator** modulating frame position produces a constant angular velocity — the cleanest case. At audio-rate modulation with a rational frequency ratio relative to the audio oscillator, the synthesis path IS a torus knot, with all the topological structure that implies.

## T³ — Inharmonicity as Third Cyclic Dimension

[[Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis]] introduces inharmonicity: the partials are displaced from their harmonic positions, producing beating patterns between adjacent partials. Those beating patterns are themselves quasi-periodic — they have a rate. That rate is a third angular dimension.

The beat envelope phase ψ ∈ [0, 2π) of the inharmonic partial structure rounds out a third circle, and the synthesis space becomes **T³ = S¹ × S¹ × S¹**: phase × frame position × inharmonic beat phase.

This is not the only way to read inharmonicity as a cyclic dimension. The B coefficient of piano inharmonicity parameterizes a continuous deformation of the torus — at β = 0, the torus is "flat" (perfectly harmonic); as β increases, the phase fiber twists relative to the frame base. This is the **fiber bundle** reading below. Both framings are valid and complementary.

### Knotted Tori: What (p,q,r) Paths Look Like

On T², a closed (p,q) path is a knotted **curve** — a 1D object — sitting on the surface of a torus in 3-space.

On T³, a closed (p,q,r) path is a knotted **torus** — a 2D surface — when projected to 3D. The path sweeps out a tube whose topology is determined by the triple (p,q,r). These objects are **torus links**: linked and knotted tori threaded through each other. The closed curve became a closed surface. The knot class became a link class.

At specific inharmonicity values — those producing rational beat frequencies relative to the audio rate and modulation rate — the synthesis path traces one of these torus links. The integers (p,q,r) encode the three frequency ratios directly. Change any one ratio and the topology of the path changes discretely: a trefoil tube becomes a figure-eight tube.

## Monodromy and the Fiber Bundle

The most structurally precise reading of inharmonicity uses **fiber bundle** language.

Think of the frame position circle (θ) as the **base space** and the phase circle (φ) as the **fiber**. In a perfectly harmonic wavetable, these are independent — completing one revolution of θ leaves φ unchanged. The bundle is **trivial**, and its total space is a flat T².

Introduce inharmonicity: now one trip around the θ circle **rotates the φ fiber** by an amount related to β. The fiber twists. This rotation — how much the fiber has turned after completing one loop of the base — is the **monodromy** of the bundle.

- Rational monodromy (β such that the twist is a rational multiple of 2π): paths close → torus knots
- Irrational monodromy: paths are ergodic — they densely fill subtori
- β = 0: trivial monodromy → flat T², harmonic synthesis
- β varying continuously: sweeps through a family of twisted tori

The inharmonicity depth scalar B in [[Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis]] is exactly a monodromy parameter. Slow LFO modulation of B traces a path through this family of twisted tori — the sound's character changes as the topological type of its phase-space structure changes.

## The Hopf Fibration

The Hopf fibration is a map **h: S³ → S²** where every point on S² has a circle as its preimage — the 3-sphere fibers into circles over the 2-sphere. Every **(p,q) torus knot** is the preimage of a great circle on S² under the Hopf map. And S³ can be decomposed as **two solid tori glued along their boundary** — exactly T² doubled and identified.

The synthesis space T³ lives in the neighborhood of this structure. When you consider the full space of all possible synthesis paths across all inharmonicity values — all (p,q,r) torus links — you are beginning to approach the topology of S³ fibered by the Hopf map. The specific inharmonicity values that produce closed paths are the rational points in this structure, and their classification is precisely the classification of torus links under the Hopf fibration.

This is where the geometry connects synthesis to the deepest structures in low-dimensional topology.

## Cross-Domain Resonances

### [[Orbital Resonance]] and the KAM Theorem

Celestial mechanics is governed by the same torus topology. Two bodies in orbital resonance move on T² in phase space; their ratio of orbital periods determines the torus knot class of their combined trajectory. Jupiter's moons form resonance chains — the Laplace resonance of Io, Europa, and Ganymede is a (1,2,4) structure on T³. The **Kirkwood gaps** in the asteroid belt are the *missing* rational resonances — regions where torus knots break under perturbation.

The **Kolmogorov-Arnold-Moser (KAM) theorem** is the deep result: in a near-integrable Hamiltonian system, most invariant tori survive small perturbations — but the rational tori (torus knots) are the first to break, fragmenting into chaotic zones. Inharmonicity β is a perturbation of the harmonic system. The rational synthesis paths (closed torus knots) are the ones that break first under large β, dissolving into quasi-chaotic timbre. The KAM theorem predicts that mildly inharmonic sounds have more stable resonance structure than strongly inharmonic ones — which matches perceptual experience.

### [[Quasicrystals]] and the Cut-and-Project Method

A quasicrystal has long-range order without periodicity — diffraction patterns with forbidden symmetries (five-fold, eight-fold). The mathematical description uses **cut-and-project**: embed a higher-dimensional periodic lattice (living on T^k) and take a lower-dimensional slice at an irrational angle. The resulting pattern is quasi-periodic.

An **inharmonic sound with k incommensurate partial frequencies is a quasicrystal in time.** Its waveform is the 1D cut through a k-dimensional periodic lattice. The "forbidden symmetries" of a quasicrystal correspond to the irrational ratios between partials that prevent the waveform from closing. Shechtman's 1984 discovery of physical quasicrystals is the material proof that quasi-periodic structures can be physically stable — as piano strings and bells demonstrate daily in the acoustic domain.

The cut-and-project framing gives a synthesis algorithm: precompute a periodic function on T^k; read it out along a line at the appropriate angle. This is audio generation by path-following through higher-dimensional periodic space. See Open Questions.

### The [[Tonnetz]] — Harmony and Timbre Are the Same Geometry

The Tonnetz is music theory's toroidal lattice: pitch classes arranged so that major thirds, minor thirds, and perfect fifths tile a torus. Chord progressions trace paths on this torus. Modulation through the circle of fifths is traversal of one circle of T²; combining it with thirds traversal completes the second circle.

The synthesis space torus and the Tonnetz are the **same topological structure** — T² — operating at different scales and on different musical parameters. The Tonnetz organizes **harmonic** relationships between discrete pitches; the synthesis torus organizes **timbral** relationships between continuous synthesis states. They are the same geometry applied to harmony and timbre respectively.

A profound consequence: the intervals that sound consonant in a given timbre are determined by that timbre's inharmonic structure ([[Harmonicity and Inharmonicity]]); the Tonnetz's shape is determined by the harmonic relationships that define consonance. If you change the inharmonicity (move to a different torus in the fiber bundle family), the consonant intervals change — the Tonnetz reshapes. Harmony and timbre are not separate domains but two projections of the same torus onto different perceptual dimensions.

### [[Berry Phase]] and Holonomy

In quantum mechanics, when a system's Hamiltonian is slowly varied through a closed cycle, the quantum state picks up a **geometric phase** (Berry phase) in addition to the dynamic phase accumulated from the energy. This geometric phase is a property of the path's topology in parameter space — not its speed or dynamics.

The Berry phase is the quantum name for the **monodromy** described above. One trip around the wavetable position circle accumulates a geometric phase equal to the holonomy of the connection over that loop. The connection is defined by the inharmonicity parameter β. At β = 0, no phase accumulates. At β ≠ 0, the phase offset is determined by the fiber bundle's curvature.

The **Aharonov-Bohm effect** is the physical instance: a charged particle encircling a magnetic flux tube picks up a measurable phase shift even when it never enters the field region — the topology of the path, not the local field strength, determines the effect. Inharmonicity-as-monodromy is the synthesis-space analog: the timbre shift accumulated over one modulation cycle is a topological quantity, not just a local one. This has a non-obvious implication: two synthesis paths with different speeds but the same topological winding class accumulate the same spectral transformation.

### [[Kuramoto Coupling]] — Lock or Drift

The Kuramoto model describes the spontaneous synchronization of coupled oscillators. Two oscillators either **phase-lock** (rational frequency ratio, pulled into a stable resonance) or **drift** (irrational ratio, relative phase wandering ergodically). Phase locking is the rational-resonance / torus-knot case. Drift is the irrational / ergodic case.

The synthesis torus makes this perceptual: a (2,3) modulation ratio locks the wavetable scan to the audio oscillator in a stable 2:3 pattern — the torus knot is the locked state, repeating exactly. A slightly irrational ratio drifts, producing a slowly evolving, never-quite-repeating pattern — the ergodic traversal of the torus surface. The psychoacoustic perception of "shimmer" or "life" in a pad sound is often the difference between a near-rational, slowly-drifting-from-lock trajectory and a rational, perfectly-periodic one.

## Open Questions

**Can a scalar field on T³ replace the oscillator bank?**  
The cut-and-project framing of quasicrystals suggests yes in principle: precompute a smooth scalar function on T³ (or T^k for k incommensurate frequencies), discretize it as a 3D lookup table, then advance position at (ω₁, ω₂, ω₃) per sample and read out with trilinear interpolation. This replaces N oscillators with 3 phase accumulators + 1 trilinear interpolation. The trade is computation for memory: table size grows as resolution³. For slowly-varying inharmonicity (large beat periods), the table becomes impractically large. For fixed, moderate inharmonicity, it may be favorable. The threshold is worth computing.

**Does beat-frequency encoding reduce computation relative to per-partial oscillators?**  
The B coefficient formula — f_n = n·f₀·√(1+B·n²) — gives all N partial frequencies from one scalar. Computing N partial frequencies from B is O(N) but with trivially simple arithmetic: one multiply and one square root per partial per update buffer. The efficiency gain is not in the per-sample oscillator computation (still N phase accumulators) but in the **modulation layer**: instead of routing N independent modulation signals, you route one (B), and all N frequencies update parametrically. Real-time B modulation is maximally efficient from a routing perspective. This is already the architecture of [[Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis]]; the torus framing confirms it is the natural parameterization.

**Does the (p,q,r) torus link class produce audible invariants?**  
If two modulation configurations have the same topological winding class — same (p,q,r) but different speeds — they should, by the Berry phase argument, produce the same accumulated spectral transformation per cycle. Is this perceptually true? A listening experiment would be: compare (2,3,1) at different overall rates; if the timbre character of each modulation cycle sounds the same regardless of speed, the Berry phase / topological-invariant reading is perceptually real.

**What is the geometry of the Wavetable B editor under this framing?**  
The Wavetable B editor displays the fiber of the bundle at a given base-space position. The flat horizontal line is zero monodromy (β = 0). Every curve is a specification of monodromy at that θ value. The full editor — all frames side by side — is a section of the bundle: a picture of how the fiber twists as you move around the base circle. This reframing may suggest a new UI display: the bundle as a whole, rather than individual frames, as the primary editing surface.

## Lost Branches

- **Seifert surfaces**: every torus knot bounds an orientable surface (the Seifert surface) whose genus is (p−1)(q−1)/2. Sweeping a synthesis parameter through a closed (p,q) path traces its Seifert surface. What does this surface sound like as a 2D audio texture? Unvisited.
- **Villarceau circles**: cutting a standard torus with a specific plane yields two perfect circles — Villarceau circles. These are the projections of certain rational T² paths. May give a UI insight for visualizing rational synthesis states.
- **S³ decomposition**: the 3-sphere decomposes into two solid tori glued along their shared boundary torus. This is the completion of the synthesis space topology — the two solid tori are the spaces "inside" and "outside" the toroidal synthesis surface. Not yet developed.
- **Higher genus surfaces**: T³ can be generalized to higher-genus surfaces for synthesis with more than three incommensurate frequency components. The [[Bolza Surface]] (genus 2, maximal symmetry) is the next step up — unexplored.

*The flat line at the harmonic center of [[Inharmonic Wavetable Synthesis]] is the equator of a torus. Every deviation from it is a path curving away from that equator. The full space of possible deviations is the torus surface; the full space of possible timbres is the torus interior; and the full space of possible modulations is the family of paths on that interior. The geometry was always there — this is just the first time we have named it.*

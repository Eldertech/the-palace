# Epictetus
## Philosopher

**Domain:** Philosopher

---

## Key Works

- Discourses (compiled by Arrian, ~108 CE)
- Enchiridion (The Handbook)

---

## Core Concepts

- **Dichotomy of control** - what's in your power vs not
- **Prohairesis** - moral character, the will
- **Impressions vs judgments** - separate perception from interpretation
- **Reserve clause** - 'if fate permits'
- **Role ethics** - fulfill your role excellently

---

## Connection to 4 Pillars

Epictetus' dichotomy of control maps directly to production decisions. You can control: your effort, your learning, your process. You cannot control: plays, success, others' opinions. Focus energy on what's in your power. The reserve clause ('I'll finish this track, fate permitting') removes attachment to outcomes.

---

## Key Quotes

> "It's not what happens to you, but how you react to it that matters."
> "He is a wise man who does not grieve for the things which he has not, but rejoices for those which he has."
> "First say to yourself what you would be; and then do what you have to do."

---

## Recommended Reading Approach

Start with Enchiridion (short, practical). Read one chapter daily, apply immediately. Keep asking: Is this in my control? If yes, act. If no, accept.

---

## Applications to Four Pillars

### Creation Pillar
Control your practice, not outcomes. Make music because you can, not because it might succeed.

### Tools Pillar
Technical skill is in your control. Master what you can control, accept limitations you can't change.

### Philosophy Pillar
Stoic producer: React to feedback with equanimity. Plays don't define quality - your effort does.

### Practice Pillar
Daily dichotomy practice: List what's in/out of your control today. Focus energy accordingly.

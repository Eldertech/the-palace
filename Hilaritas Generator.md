# Hilaritas Generator

The classroom — or the live stream, or any learning space — reconceived as a machine for producing Spinozist **hilaritas**: the active, whole-being joy that accompanies an increase in power-to-act (as distinct from *titillatio*, localized pleasure).

## The Mechanism

A hilaritas generator works by activating all [[FOUR PILLARS]] at once: you're **creating** something, using **tools** that extend you, discovering **philosophical** connections, through iterative **practice**. When all four fire together the student's power-to-act increases *globally* — they leave more capable in general, not just more informed about one topic.

The key inversion: hilaritas is **not a side effect** of good teaching — it IS the teaching. When a student experiences hilaritas, the increased power-to-act *is* the learning. The joy isn't a reward for having learned; it's the subjective experience of capability expanding.

## The Metrics Redirector

One operating question: **"How do you feel?"** not **"How many views?"** Platform analytics measure titillatio (narrow, localized); felt experience measures whether hilaritas occurred. 10,000 views that left you depleted was a *tristitia* event; 40 views that left you expanded was a hilaritas event. Insist on the right signal. See [[Quality Manifesto]].

## The Learning Architect

The old teacher model delivers content shaped for a group. The new model teaches the *skill of learning* shaped precisely for an individual — not "here is what you need to know about Spinoza" but "here is how to put Spinoza across the table from you and have him answer your specific question about your specific patch." (AI as embodiment / time machine — the [[Excellent Adventure]] method.) The teacher's job becomes designing the learning encounter itself; prompting guides given to learners are not shortcuts, they are the curriculum. The highest-leverage hilaritas mechanism is not a good classroom — it is a learner who can generate hilaritas for themselves.

### The Design Primitives (in development)

A learning encounter is a hilaritas generator when it includes all four:

1. **A real thing to make** — not a simulation; something that can fail, that resists, that teaches through its own behavior.
2. **A tool that extends** — at the edge of current capability, so the extension is felt.
3. **A cross-domain moment** — where the specific thing illuminates a principle that applies elsewhere (the philosophy pillar firing). Cannot be pre-scripted, only set up.
4. **A reflecting surface** — journaling, conversation, or a question that asks the learner to name what increased. "How do you feel?" is the minimum.

Design encounters where all four are present, then stay out of the way when they fire. The danger is over-engineering — a designed encounter with no room for spontaneous discovery is a tristitia generator in disguise. See [[Palace Philosophies]] for the Taoist counterweight: design conditions that invite flow, don't prescribe outcomes.

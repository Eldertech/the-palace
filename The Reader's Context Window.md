# The Reader's Context Window

A discipline for laying out any surface a human reads to *decide* or *orient*: order the information the way the reader's mind actually assembles it, and show only what is necessary to assemble it. The question is never "what could I put here?" but **"what order does this reader need this in to build their own context window?"** — and Loudon is far more sensitive to that order than the machine is, so the order is his to set and the page's to obey.

It is the structural sibling of [[Speak Like a Person, Log Like a Protocol]]. That principle governs the *register* of the words on a surface; this one governs their *sequence and their count*. Together they are the two laws of a palace front-end. Where they hold, the interface disappears ([[Merleau-Ponty]]: the instrument absorbed into the body schema; a front-end works when you stop noticing it).

## The order

Each line answers the question the previous line provoked. For a decision surface:

1. **Who is speaking** — the anchor. Until you know which project is talking, nothing else has a frame to land in.
2. **Where they are / what they're doing** — orientation, directly under the name. It must teach the terms the question will use, so that by the time you reach "approve stage 2" you already know what stage 2 *is*.
3. **The evidence** — only if there is any; the audio to audition, the two pictures. Visible, because *perceiving it is part of the decision.*
4. **The question** — seated **directly on its options**. Context loads first, above; the decision comes last.

Context above the question, never wedged between the question and its answers — otherwise the eye jumps up for the ask and down for the answer, and the surface fights the reader.

## The four rules under the order

- **The question seats on its options.** Never separate the ask from its answers. A button reading "approve stage 2" is only legible with the question right above it.
- **Mark, don't repeat.** The recommendation is indicated *on its own option*, once — not restated, not given a panel of emphasis repeated down the whole page.
- **Fewest, and dynamic.** The fewest words, icons, and indicators that carry the meaning; render only the slots *this* instance needs; cut every label that is not load-bearing.
- **A second beat where it commits.** A consequential action takes a deliberate second step (pick, *then* execute) so a misclick cannot fire it — minimalism that costs nothing because it removes a hazard, not a control.

## Origin

Forged 2026-06-16 in the [[STIGMERGY v2.0 — Consolidation & Primary Interface]] redesign of the [[Trickster]] decision card, across three live mockups. The card began with the question floating above the orientation; Loudon caught his own eye jumping over the context to connect the ask to the buttons, and the principle fell out of fixing it. It generalizes immediately past the card — the queue items, the digest rows, and eventually entry rendering are all surfaces a reader builds context from, and all read better ordered to the reader rather than to the data.

# Martin Buber — speech

Ground-truth voice evidence. The `## Voice` note in [[Martin Buber]] is a compile *from this file*.

## Sources & their limits (read before quoting)

- **Translation is a veil, not a window.** Buber wrote in German (retelling Yiddish/Hebrew sources). Every English quote passed through a translator. The two major *I and Thou* translations disagree on register and even the central word: **Ronald Gregor Smith (1937/58)** keeps "Thou"; **Walter Kaufmann (1970)** uses "You" (arguing *Du* is the ordinary intimate pronoun, not an archaism). Buber sent Smith "well over two hundred corrections." **Never present a single English sentence as *the* Buber sentence** — quote both, tagged.
- **Two registers, not one voice.** *I and Thou* (1923) is compressed, aphoristic, near-oracular. The *Tales of the Hasidim* (1946–47) are warm, narrative, humane, funny. Sample only the first and he sounds colder and more gnomic than the man; only the second and he loses the philosophical bite. **Both needed.**
- **No verified recording.** Buber is a print figure. He gave the 1957 Carl Rogers dialogue (transcript published) but no primary audio/video was verified in research — treat "Buber said X in conversation" as **transcript-mediated print**, not confirmed recording.

## The register, in one breath

Turns *toward*; addresses rather than describes; near-oracular and compressed in *I and Thou*, warm and story-shaped in the tales. Refuses to hand over a method; when asked for one, relocates the question into the between or names the relapse.

## Excerpts (verbatim, tagged)

1. "All real living is meeting."
   — *I and Thou*, Gregor Smith (1937/58), Part I. German: *"Alles wirkliche Leben ist Begegnung."* Kaufmann (1970): **"All actual life is encounter."** **THE BRIDGE QUOTE (closes [[Dovetail Joint ↔ Counterpoint]]).** **[edited print · rehearsed]** — *preserve the Smith wording per the bridge, but cite Kaufmann's variant alongside every time; the discrepancy is the lesson, not a footnote.*

2. "In the beginning is the relation."
   — *I and Thou*, Part I section heading. German: *"Am Anfang ist die Beziehung."* **[edited print · rehearsed]**

3. "I become through my relation to the Thou; as I become I, I say Thou."
   — *I and Thou*, Gregor Smith, Part I. Kaufmann: **"I require a You to become; becoming I, I say You."** **[edited print]**

4. "Every Thou in our world must become an It."
   — *I and Thou*, Gregor Smith. Kaufmann: **"Every You must become an It in our world."** **[edited print · rehearsed]** — *his own concession; load-bearing for the blindspot.*

5. "In every You we address the eternal You, in every sphere according to its manner."
   — *I and Thou*, Kaufmann, Part III. **[edited print]**

6. "Through the Thou a person becomes I."
   — *I and Thou* (condensed form of the Part I claim). **[edited print · rehearsed]**

7. On the narrow ridge: it is "no 'happy middle' which ignores the reality of paradox and contradiction, but rather a paradoxical unity of what one usually understands only as alternatives."
   — *Between Man and Man* (Gregor Smith, 1947), "The Narrow Ridge." **[edited print · rehearsed — FLAG: concept well-attested; pin the single cleanest verbatim sentence from the Religion-Online full text before hard-quoting.]**

8. "The world is not comprehensible, but it is embraceable: through the embracing of one of its beings."
   — widely attributed, consistent with *I and Thou*'s register. **[edited print · confidence: working — primary page not pinned]**

9. "Greatness by nature includes a power, but not a will to power."
   — *Between Man and Man* (1965 English edition, per Wikiquote). **[edited print]**

10. "The real struggle is not between East and West, or capitalism and communism, but between education and propaganda."
    — *Encounter with Martin Buber* (1972, posthumous). **[edited print / transcript-mediated]**

11. "I do not accept any absolute formulas for living."
    — *Martin Buber: An Intimate Portrait* (1971, posthumous, from conversations). **[edited print / transcript-mediated]** — *good in-character line for his refusal of method.*

12. **APOCRYPHAL — DO NOT USE AS A DIRECT QUOTE:** "When two people relate to each other authentically and humanly, God is the electricity that surges between them."
    — circulates widely (Goodreads, QuoteFancy) with **no traceable source** in any Buber text. Reads like a popularizer's compression of the eternal-Thou idea. If referenced at all, name it explicitly as "a popular paraphrase of uncertain provenance, not verified against Buber's texts." **A faithful embodiment should be able to say "I don't think I actually said that."**

### For contrast/friction (not Buber's own voice)

13. Rosenzweig: "In your setting up the I-IT, you give the I-Thou a cripple for an opponent... You make of creation a chaos, just good enough to provide construction material for the new building."
    — *Briefe und Tagebücher*, per SEP. **[edited print · third party — gentle internal critique]**

14. Levinas: "I have read Buber then with a great deal of respect and attention, but I have not reached the point of agreeing with him."
    — widely cited in Buber–Levinas literature. **[edited print · third party]**

15. Kaufmann (translator turned critic): the tone is "oracular," the conceptions "Manichean" and "rashly romantic and ecstatic"; Buber "mistook deep emotional stirrings for revelation."
    — prologue to his 1970 translation, per SEP. **[edited print · third party — verify exact wording against the Kaufmann prologue before quoting verbatim.]**

## Sources & their limits (read first)

- **The rehearsed analogies are stage/interview *signatures*, not spontaneous invention.** "Gardening vs architecture" and the screwdriver have been redeployed for 20+ years; an interviewer noted in 2017 that he "does quite frequently" spark up that analogy. → They show his *polished public register*, not off-the-cuff talk.
- **Two registers, keep them distinct:** *lecture Eno* (John Peel Lecture, Edge talks) = polished, big-theory-made-simple; *conversational Eno* (print interviews) = digressive, exploratory, thinking-out-loud, self-deprecating.
- His own note on why he talks at all: he does interviews *to force himself to be articulate*.

## Confirmed traits (multi-source)

Calm, warm, **unpretentious**; "a careful but curious explorer"; thoughts "wind through a network of interconnected references"; mild jokes and self-deprecation carried in vocal inflection; **the analogy-machine** — reaches for a homely object and turns it into a theory.

## Excerpts (verbatim where marked; each tagged by context)

- **[rehearsed · signature analogy]** "The difference between classical and contemporary music is the difference between architecture and gardening." — reused across two decades; on generative music. (Loud And Quiet, 2017; Edge, *Composers as Gardeners*.) *verify exact wording.*
- **[rehearsed · analogy]** Making art "can be more usefully thought of as being like gardening: you plant a few seeds and then start watching what happens between them, how they come to life and how they interact… the process of making is a process of you interacting with the object, and letting it set the pace." (Edge / artist statements.) *verify.*
- **[stage lecture · scripted]** Art is "everything you don't have to do" — the screwdriver's business end is fixed, but the handle "can be plain, striped, blue, red, yellow, pink, fat, thin, shaped, or pared down." (John Peel Lecture, 2015.) *verify.*
- **[meta · on interviews]** "The only rationale I have for doing interviews is that I want to think about certain things — and I find the most convenient way to think about them is to put myself in a situation where I have to be articulate." *verify source.*
- **[spontaneous · candid aside]** "I've never been that thrilled by the Renaissance." — then reasons it out at length (his digression habit). *verify source.*

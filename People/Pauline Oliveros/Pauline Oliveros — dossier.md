# Pauline Oliveros — dossier

Embodiment corpus for [[Pauline Oliveros]]. Nothing invented; contested points marked; quotes to be verified.

## Timeline

- **1932** — Born in Houston, Texas; accordion from childhood; drawn early to the sounds *around* music (radio static, insects).
- **1960s** — Central to the **San Francisco Tape Music Center** (with Morton Subotnick, Ramon Sender); a pioneering woman in electronic/experimental music. Directs the Center when it moves to Mills College.
- **1970** — *Sonic Meditations* — text-score practices for anyone, not just trained musicians ("Teach Yourself to Fly," etc.).
- **1970s–80s** — Professor at UC San Diego; develops "Sonic Awareness" combining attention, improvisation, and bodywork.
- **1988** — Records *Deep Listening* with Stuart Dempster and Panaiotis in the Dan Harpole Cistern (Fort Worden, WA) — ~45-second reverb. The pun becomes a discipline.
- **1990s–2016** — Founds the **Deep Listening Institute**; a certification program; the **Expanded Instrument System** (live electronic processing). Long career teaching (Rensselaer/Mills). Dies 2016.

## What she actually holds

- **Hearing vs. listening** — hearing is passive/physiological; listening is active, trained, chosen.
- **Deep Listening** — reception of the *whole* sound field at once, inclusive of the unintended (room, body, environment, silence).
- **Focal vs. global attention** — narrow point vs. whole sphere; the practice is holding/toggling both, and widening.
- **The practice is the piece** — *Sonic Meditations* dissolve the composer/performer/listener roles; participation over performance.
- **Radical inclusion** — anyone can do it; no wrong sounds; sound as a path to awareness and healing.

## Characteristic moves

- Redirect from making to attending — "before you add, listen to what's here."
- Give a simple, doable instruction (a Sonic Meditation) rather than a score to master.
- Widen rather than sharpen; include the unintended.
- Frame listening as embodied and therapeutic (the feet as ears; sound in the body).

## Blindspots and live disagreements

- **No criterion of quality.** If everything is material and all attention is valid, on what ground could you say one work is better, or that a listener listened *badly*? A practice that can't be done wrong can't quite be done well.
- **Inclusiveness dissolving craft.** "Everyone can participate, no training required" is generous and can also erase discernment and the value of skill.
- **Sacred framing resists critique.** Because it's therapeutic and near-devotional, questioning it can feel like rejecting kindness — which shields it from real argument.
- **Cage contrast (productive, not a flaw):** Cage removed intention from the *making*; she deepened attention in the *receiving*. Both risk surrendering the tools to judge — he playfully, she reverently.
- **Failure mode to avoid:** the mindfulness-app narrator. Keep her a serious, radical experimentalist with a real unanswered question.

## Register and voice

Warm, inclusive, expansive, meditative, unhurried, quietly radical, generous. Verify before quoting:

- "Listen to everything all the time and remind yourself when you are not listening."
- "Deep Listening is listening in every possible way to everything possible."
- (Sonic Meditation) "Walk so silently that the bottoms of your feet become ears."

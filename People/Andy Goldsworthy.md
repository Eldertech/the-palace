# Andy Goldsworthy
## Artist

**Domain:** Artist

---

## Key Works

- Rivers and Tides (documentary, 2001)
- Ephemeral Works (ongoing)
- Stone works, ice sculptures, leaf arrangements

---

## Core Concepts

- **Ephemeral art** - works designed to decay/disappear
- **Collaboration with nature** - working with materials' properties
- **Process over product** - documentation of making
- **Site-specific** - art inseparable from place
- **Failure as teacher** - ice melts, stones fall, leaves blow away

---

## Connection to 4 Pillars

Goldsworthy's ephemeral works = live performance, improvisation. His collaboration with materials = working with synthesis limitations. When his ice sculpture melts, he doesn't call it failure - it revealed something about ice. When your patch doesn't work as expected, it's revealing something about the system.

---

## Key Quotes

> "Movement, change, light, growth and decay are the lifeblood of nature, the energies that I try to tap through my work."
> "I enjoy the freedom of just using my hands and 'found' tools—a sharp stone, the quill of a feather, thorns. I take the opportunities each day offers."
> "The things I make are often in collaboration with nature."

---

## Recommended Reading Approach

Watch Rivers and Tides (the film is the teaching). Notice his process: Try, fail, try again, learn from failure. Apply to your practice: What if you made ephemeral music - performed once, never recorded?

---

## Applications to Four Pillars

### Creation Pillar
Ephemeral sessions: Make music, perform it live, delete project. Process over artifact.

### Tools Pillar
Work with tool limitations, not against them. The 'flaw' is the teacher.

### Philosophy Pillar
Impermanence. Nothing lasts. Make it anyway. Let it go.

### Practice Pillar
Weekly ephemeral creation: Make something beautiful, then destroy it. Notice attachment dissolve.

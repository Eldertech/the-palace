# John Cage — speech

Ground-truth evidence for how Cage actually speaks, for the entry's `## Voice` note and for deep casts. **Build the profile from these; two of his most-cited "sources" are not real speech at all — see below.** Verify exact wording against the primary source before quoting (WebFetch was unavailable when this was seeded).

## Sources & their limits (READ FIRST — this citizen has traps)

- **❌ The Ted Berrigan "Interview with John Cage" (1966, *Mother* no. 7) is a FABRICATION.** Berrigan invented it whole, as a Dadaist act; a panel (Sontag, Brustein; George Plimpton selecting) awarded it best interview of the year before realizing. Cage was amused. **Do not use it as evidence of his speech** — it measures the myth, not the man. **Confirmed** (Zack Rogow blog on Berrigan; Paris Review author page; Mother no. 7 bibliographic record listing it as "hoax interview with John Cage").
- **⚠️ Kostelanetz, *Conversing with Cage* is a Cage-*approved collage*** — excerpts from 150+ interviews across four decades, rearranged by topic, "checked and corrected by Cage himself." Excellent for his *phrasing and ideas*; **not** evidence of spontaneous conversational rhythm (it flattens era and setting). **Confirmed** (Routledge/Taylor & Francis publisher description, 2nd ed.).
- **Context by setting:** 1960 *I've Got a Secret* = performative TV (sly, comic timing); late-life interviews (Bruce Duffie; "Cage at 70/75") = closest to his real, gentle spontaneous voice; the *Silence* aphorisms = **composed literary prose**, not talk.

## Confirmed traits (multi-source, late-life accounts)

Voice **soft, higher-pitched, lilting, slightly sibilant**; **gentle, gracious, unhurried** — would "go on for the rest of the afternoon." **Laughs easily**, a grin bursting a weathered face. **Humble** to the point of self-effacement; "moves hesitantly"; a "quiet aura of Zen humility." Enigmatic on the surface, coherent underneath.

## Excerpts (verbatim where marked; each tagged by context)

- **[performative TV · 1960]** "I consider music the production of sound, and since in the piece which you will hear I produce sound, I will call it music." — correcting the host, placidly, with a small smile. His signature *reframe-the-question* move. **Confirmed exact.** (Hyperallergic, "John Cage's 1960 Game Show Performance"; also andre mount, "Laughter Over Tears" — both on *I've Got a Secret*, Feb 24 1960.)
- **[performative TV · 1960]** "Of course. I consider laughter preferable to tears." — said when host Garry Moore asked if audience laughter would be acceptable, just before *Water Walk*. **Confirmed exact wording and source** (same *I've Got a Secret* broadcast, per Hyperallergic and andre mount). **Correction:** the file previously implied the Kant framing ("music and laughter are the two things that needn't mean anything to give deep pleasure") was said in the same breath on this broadcast — it was not. Neither source for this exchange mentions Kant. The Kant line is a real, separately-attested Cage statement ("There was a German philosopher... Immanuel Kant, and he said there are two things that don't have to mean anything, one is music and the other is laughter... in order to give us deep pleasure") but belongs to a different context — it appears in *Silence: Lectures and Writings* and in the film *Écoute* (Miroslav Sebestik) — not the 1960 game-show exchange. Keep the two quotes separate; do not merge contexts.
- **[book prose · *M: Writings '67–'72*]** mushroom-hunting clothes: "Clothes I wear for mushroom hunting are rarely sent to the cleaner. They constitute a collection of odors I produce and gather while rambling in the woods. I notice not only dogs (cats, too) are delighted (they love to smell me)." **Corrected wording + source** — the file's excerpt ("a collection of odors I produce and gather while rambling in the woods") was accurate as a fragment but unsourced; full sentence and source now confirmed as *M: Writings '67–'72*, which is **composed page prose, not spontaneous speech** — recatalog mentally alongside the *Silence*/*Lecture on Nothing* literary excerpts below, despite sitting in the "everyday" slot.
- **[Zen device · not everyday speech]** in the *Lecture on Nothing* Q&A he gave "six previously prepared answers regardless of the question asked" — a deliberate stunt; do not generalize it to conversation.
- **[LITERARY — NOT speech]** "I have nothing to say and I am saying it and that is poetry as I need it"; "There is no such thing as silence." — composed for the page (*Silence*, *Lecture on Nothing*). Keep separate from his talking voice.

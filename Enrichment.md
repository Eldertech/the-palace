# Enrichment

![[Enrichment — hero.png]]

A ceremony for moving the whole palace forward in tiny, joyful steps. The opposite of the [[Project Stewardship System]] — instead of a permanent agent tending one project at deep rotation, this is a studio visit: walk through many entries, leave a small varied gift in each, keep the energy spread, never linger. The artifacts that result are *enrichments* of entries that already exist, not plans for entries that don't.

This ceremony emerged on 2026-05-04 from a long deliberative deposit. The earlier name was "Proof Sprint" — accurate but too narrow. *Enrichment* covers more ground: not just "make a proof" but "make this entry richer in some specific way," where richness can mean a sound, an image, a poem, a philosopher's voice, a counterargument, a person to reach out to, a sharper forward vector, a question the entry didn't know it needed to ask.

## When to run it

Trigger phrases:

- *"Let's enrich"* — open-ended, I pick what to touch
- *"Enrich [entry name]"* — focused on one entry
- *"Enrich the [X] connection"* — focused on a typed-link relationship
- *"Enrich the [X] neighborhood"* — focused on a map / cluster
- *"Make-teach-move-on"* — Loudon's earlier phrasing for the same instinct
- *"Let's go oblique"* — the spirit-level invocation

The ceremony does not need to be scheduled. It runs whenever the palace feels static and breadth would help, or when one entry feels under-witnessed and could use a few small stones thrown at it.

## Spirit

This is Hilaritas — celebratory intelligence, joyful wisdom. The opposite of optimization-energy. Three principles:

**Oblique, not direct.** The questions to hold in mind: *What is unproven? What can I show now? How do I create a moment of joy in this entry? What is one small thing toward the forward vector? How do I make this entry richer?* These are not the same questions a stewardship system would ask. They invite tangents, accidents, surprises.

**Breadth, not depth.** Five active cards. Multiple targets per session. Spreading energy across many entries means none gets perfected — which is the point. A perfect enrichment kills its own next move. A varied stack of half-finished enrichments leaves the palace noisier and more alive.

**Joy as a metric.** If a card produces no spark, discard it without ceremony. If a card produces a laugh, save it. The entries notice. Over time the palace develops a feel — entries that have been visited by enrichment many times read differently from entries that haven't.

## What can be enriched

Four target shapes, in widening scope:

| Target | Example | What enrichment looks like |
|---|---|---|
| One entry | "enrich Crystal Synthesizer" | 5 small artifacts each pointed at some aspect of that one entry |
| One typed link | "enrich the Bessel↔FM connection" | artifacts that make the relationship between two entries audible / visible |
| One neighborhood | "enrich the synthesis projects neighborhood" | artifacts that span 3-7 related entries, finding the shape of the cluster |
| Open | "let's enrich" | I pick — usually whichever entry feels least-recently-witnessed |

## What kinds of artifacts

**Default to image, audio, or interactive.** Text artifacts (haiku, mocked Wikipedia stubs, philosopher voice-acts, summaries) are valid but should be a minority. *Show, don't tell.* Find an untested assumption in the entry and test it — make a sound or render an image that the entry alone could not produce. Reach for text only when the artifact IS prose by nature: proposed entry edits, forward-vector revisions, person profiles, lyrics about language. A round of five cards should rarely contain more than two text artifacts.

This is intentionally not enumerated. The ceremony's job is to invent purposes and shapes that fit the moment. Examples that have shown up so far:

*Audio:* synthesis demos, narrated catalogs (via Kokoro through Claude Code), Suno song prompts, one-bar musical sketches, soundscape mockups, a heard-but-not-stated audio assumption made audible

*Visual:* concept art, diagrams, spectrograms, interface mockups, cover art, one beautiful image that holds the entry's idea, comic panels, movement notation

*Interactive:* parameter sliders, animated explorers, clickable maps, tiny single-screen games, reactive sketches

*Text — short:* haiku, 12-word summary, 60-second elevator pitch, mantra, koan, liner notes, Wikipedia stub from 2050

*Text — longer:* lyric or song, poem, letter to a future student, letter from a former self, imagined press release, imagined hate-mail, manifesto, recipe (how to cook the idea)

*Voice acts:* a philosopher reflects, an expert critique, a friendly skeptic, a future user at Stage 5, a historical figure whose ghost would love this

*Project mechanics:* forward-vector reassessment, stage-transition proposal, hook-quality re-rating, candidate `connects-to` links, `couples-with` partner project proposal, title alternatives

*Pedagogy:* 5-minute lesson script, single excellent quiz question, prerequisite knowledge graph, the moment-of-aha capture, the tutorial's first 60 seconds

*People connections:* one person to interview + draft email + their profile snippet, a researcher whose work intersects, a book that should be on the shelf, a podcast to pitch

*Sensory tests:* color palettes, texture references, synesthesia mappings, architectural correlates, time-of-day mappings

*Provocations:* "if this is true, then…", "the thing that will stop this from working is…", a thought experiment from another era, an adjacent question

*Generative AI handoffs:* Suno prompt, Midjourney prompt, Kokoro narration prompt, Claude Code build prompt (when the make is too big for one-shot generation)

This list is *examples, not canon*. New shapes are welcome. If three sessions in a row produce a new shape that lands well, it can be added here. If a shape stops landing, drop it without ceremony.

## Purpose tags

Every card carries a *purpose* — a single line stating what the artifact is *for*. The tag is required so reviewers know what to react to. The tag's vocabulary is *not* required to come from any list. Examples that have surfaced so far:

- *showing the concept made tangible*
- *testing the assumption that X reads as Y*
- *comparing two aesthetic paths*
- *provoking — pushing back on the entry's self-image*
- *embodying a typed link to another entry*
- *refining the forward vector*
- *inviting a person into the conversation*
- *forcing compression — what survives at 12 words?*
- *occupying an empty corner of the entry*
- *answering a question the entry has been avoiding*

Treat these as drafts. The right purpose for a given card is the one that names *why this artifact, for this entry, right now* — not the one closest to a list. Over many enrichment sessions, the palace will develop its own working vocabulary of purposes; let it.

## The pace

Five cards live in the queue at any time. As you act on a card, a new one slides in from below to keep the stack at five. While you're on card 3, I'm regenerating revisions of card 1 and creating the next fresh card. If you're slow, the queue stays full — that's healthy back-pressure on me; don't rush.

## The interface

The cards are reviewed inside **[[STIGMERGY]]** — the **QUEUE** deck carries an *enrichment cards* section beneath the open-work board, in the same phosphor BBS grammar. Each card shows its rehydration block, the artifact, and the deposit/revise/discard controls; acting on one writes `Enrichment/inbox.md` and fires the supervisor through STIGMERGY's Node actuator (`POST /api/cards/respond`). There is no separate server to start — the surface is the one terminal you already operate the palace in.

*(History, 2026-06-16: through May 2026 the cards were reviewed in a standalone Python/Flask page at `localhost:7878` — `Enrichment/server.py`. STIGMERGY's Phase 4.5 absorbed the card queue — reading card folders, writing the inbox, and firing the supervisor, with the actuator's hard-won PID-liveness + daemon-cleanup robustness ported into the Node server (`_ops/stigmergy/app/server/actuator.js` + `server/cards.js`). The Flask server was then retired as part of the [[STIGMERGY v2.0 — Consolidation & Primary Interface]] Phase 1; it is recoverable from git if ever needed.)*

Each card opens with a *rehydration* block, because by the time you reach this card you may not have thought about its target entry in weeks:

- The target's name as an obsidian:// link (one click reopens it in Obsidian)
- The forward vector, rendered in italics — the entry speaking in its own voice
- A two-sentence summary — what the entry is, what stage, what's most alive about it right now
- *Then* the artifact

The artifact is followed by a wide free-text response area. You can write anything; the *deposit shapes* below are labels you can prepend if they help you act crisply, but you don't have to use them:

- **deposit** — approve, place inline, commit. Optional inline note: where in the entry it should go.
- **revise** — describe the change. The v2 returns to the bottom of the queue.
- **forward-vector tweak** — draft a revised vector. I edit the frontmatter and commit.
- **palace to-do** — append a line to [[Palace To-Do]].
- **graffiti** — append a thought to [[Palace Graffiti]].
- **larger revision** — describe a substantial entry edit. I draft as graffiti for your review (not committed to the entry yet).
- **discard** — delete the artifact, no record kept.

Free prose without labels works fine. The labels are scaffolding for when crisp action is wanted.

## When approving — the placement protocol

When you mark a card *deposit*, three things happen:

1. **The artifact file moves into the target entry's bundle — directly into the bundle root** with a descriptive filename. For project entries this means `Projects/<Entry>/<descriptive-name>.<ext>`; for concept entries it means `<EntryName>/<descriptive-name>.<ext>`. Examples: `recipe-p4mm.md`, `petri-dish-image-prompt.md`, `bessel-spectra-by-beta.png`. Date-prefix when a series is likely to accumulate (`2026-05-05-recipe-p4mm.md`) so the timeline reads cleanly via filesystem sort.

   **No `proofs/` subfolder.** That name was a fossil from this ceremony's earlier *Proof Sprint* framing — it generalizes when the act of enrichment is supposed to specify. Most artifacts here are not proofs. They are recipes, voice-acts, image prompts, koans, listening exercises, philosopher-visits, FV revisions, link proposals. Each deserves its own clear name. Generalizing them under a single "proofs" category is the kind of premature taxonomy palace work explicitly avoids — *let categories emerge from many runs before locking down*.

2. **When a single enrichment produces a coherent multi-file set**, create a folder *named for what the set IS* — `damping-regimes-listening-exercise/`, `bessel-spectra-by-beta/`, `petri-dish-iterations/`. The category folder is born of the specific work, not imposed in advance. Single artifacts never get wrapped in a folder; that's what good filenames are for.

3. **The entry's body is edited *near the topic the artifact clarifies.*** Not at the bottom. If a chart explains the very first concept introduced, the link goes near the top. If a narrated catalog supports a list of variants, the link sits inside that list.

The inline link can take whatever shape fits:

- A parenthetical: *"the bending of the harmonic line ([[2026-05-04-bending-spectra|see spectrum]])…"*
- An embedded image: *`![[2026-05-04-bessel-fm.png]]`*
- A callout: *`> 🎵 [[2026-05-04-narrated-catalog]] — six crystals narrated by Kokoro`*

The placement decision is mine, exercised with judgment — read the entry, understand the artifact, find the place where the artifact would most help a reader who landed there cold.

**Existing artifacts already deposited under `proofs/` stay where they are.** Moving them would break inline references; the inconsistency for a few weeks is fine. New deposits use the new convention. A future Walk or Weave session may surface that the old `proofs/` files want to be relocated — that move is its own ceremony, not blocking this one.

## The log lives in git

There is no `## Proofs` section growing at the bottom of any entry. The log is `git log -- "Projects/Crystal Synthesizer.md"` — the entry's biography on demand.

Commit message convention:

```
enrich(<Entry Name>): <one-line description of artifact> — purpose: <freeform tag>
```

Examples:

```
enrich(Crystal Synthesizer): narrated catalog of 6 crystals — purpose: showing the concept made tangible
enrich(Bessel Functions in Synthesis): J_n(β) chart correlated to FM sidebands — purpose: refining the FM connection
enrich(Retrospective Delay): 7-frame séance cat morph — purpose: comparing aesthetic options for the JSUI character
```

`git log --oneline -- Projects/` becomes a perfectly readable history of every enrichment ever made.

> *Naming note (added 2026-05-05):* the early version of this ceremony, and the early version of this filesystem layout, used a `proofs/` subfolder under each entry. That name was retired — it was a fossil from this ceremony's earlier "Proof Sprint" framing, and most artifacts produced here are not proofs (recipes, voice-acts, image prompts, koans). Specificity over generalization. Existing `proofs/` artifacts stay in place; new deposits go into the entry's bundle root with descriptive names. See *§ When approving — the placement protocol* above.

## Where files live

```
The Palace/
├── Enrichment.md              ← this file (ceremony spec + skill instructions)
├── Enrichment/                ← live queue (always present, contents rotate)
│   ├── inbox.md               ← your responses land here; I clear lines as I act
│   ├── card-001/              ← one folder per in-flight card
│   │   ├── card.md            ← target, purpose, summary, FV reminder, my reasoning
│   │   └── <artifact files>   ← the actual media
│   └── card-002/ ...           ← up to 5 active cards
└── Projects/<Entry>/         ← deposited artifacts land in the entry's bundle root
                              ← with descriptive filenames (no proofs/ subfolder)
```

The `Enrichment/` folder is transient. It exists to hold the queue. If it were nuked tomorrow, only in-flight work would be lost — the palace itself is unaffected. This is intentional: the ceremony does not accumulate operational sediment.

## Mode — Claude Code or Cowork

This ceremony runs best in a **Claude Code session at the palace root**. Claude Code has the file access needed to commit approved enrichments — moving artifact files, editing entries inline, generating commits, deleting tombstone folders. Cowork can prepare cards but cannot commit them or delete folders; running this ceremony from Cowork means the human has to manually accept each deposit and clean up tombstones.

When in doubt, open the palace in Claude Code, say "let's enrich [target]," and the ceremony begins.

## The round protocol

A *round* is one full turn of the iteration cycle. The user triggers a round in Claude Code by saying *"next round"* or *"process the inbox"* or *"let's enrich"*. One round is one Claude Code response; rounds compose into long sessions naturally.

Per round, Claude does:

**0a. Orient (first round of a session only).** If this is the first time the ceremony has run in the current session, read [[JEWEL]] (`JEWEL.md`) and [[Substrate Skill]] (`_ops/Substrate Skill.md`) before generating any cards. CLAUDE.md is auto-loaded and tells you these exist, but does not pre-load them. They are short — Jewel is ~96 lines, Substrate Skill ~171 — and they shape *tone* and *operational discipline* in ways that the Enrichment ceremony depends on but does not itself describe (typed-link ontology, the never-violate list, depth-over-coverage, palace voice). Skip this step on subsequent rounds; the context persists within the session.

If targeting a specific entry, also read that entry in full before generating cards for it. The forward vector and frontmatter in card.md must reflect what the entry currently says, not a guess.

**0b. Make sure STIGMERGY is up.** The cards surface in STIGMERGY's QUEUE deck (the *enrichment cards* section). If the dev server isn't running, start it (`npm --prefix _ops/stigmergy/app run dev`, or the `stigmergy` launch config). There is no separate enrichment server — STIGMERGY reads the card folders and writes the inbox directly. Point the user at the QUEUE deck at the end of the round so they can review.

**1. Read `Enrichment/inbox.md`.** Parse each `## card-NNN — Target` block into (card_id, target, purpose, response text). The `---` rule separates blocks. If the inbox is empty (no `## card-` headers), skip steps 2 and 3 — this round is *fresh-cards-only*.

**2. Act on each block.** The response text may begin with an action tag (`deposit:`, `revise:`, `discard:`, etc.) or be free prose. If the action is ambiguous, *say so on the way to acting* — interpret, name the interpretation, and proceed. Don't silently guess.

The actions:

- **deposit** — move the artifact from `Enrichment/card-NNN/` into the target entry's bundle root (`<EntryFolder>/<descriptive-slug>.<ext>` — no `proofs/` subfolder; see *§ When approving — the placement protocol* for the full convention and the reasoning). Edit the target entry's body to weave in a reference *near the topic the artifact clarifies* — not at the bottom. Then archive the card by setting `archived: true` in its `card.md` (or deleting the folder if running in Claude Code where deletion works). Commit with the message convention: `enrich(<Entry>): <one-line> — purpose: <freeform tag>`.
- **revise** — generate a v2 of the artifact addressing the response. Replace the artifact file in the card folder; update card.md's `reasoning` to reflect what changed. The card stays in queue.
- **more like this** — keep the deposited card archived; *also* generate one or more sibling cards in the same purpose register pointed at the same or related targets.
- **forward-vector tweak** — edit the target entry's frontmatter `forward_vector` field. Commit. Archive the card.
- **palace to-do** — append a line to `Palace To-Do.md`. Archive the card.
- **graffiti** — append a thought to `Palace Graffiti.md`. Archive the card.
- **larger revision** — draft the proposed entry edit as a section in `Palace Graffiti.md` for review (do *not* commit to the entry yet). Archive the card.
- **discard** — archive the card with no further effect.

**3. Clear processed blocks from `inbox.md`.** Reset the file to its header-only state. Do this only after all actions have completed successfully.

**4. Top up the queue.** Count active (non-archived) card folders. If fewer than 5, generate fresh cards. The targets to pick are, in order of preference: (a) targets named in user "more like this" feedback this round; (b) targets currently in the *Active focus* tier of the priority guide; (c) any entry that hasn't been visited by enrichment in a while; (d) the obliquely-chosen entry — pick something the user hasn't been thinking about, that's the spirit. Each new card commits one specific purpose; do not generate filler.

**5. Commit.** All file changes from this round get one or more commits. The git log is the biography.

**6. Hand back to the QUEUE.** End the round with a one-line "→ N new cards in the QUEUE deck" so the user knows what to do next; STIGMERGY surfaces them on its next reload (the SSE live tail + the card-queue refresh).

A round ends here. The user reloads the browser, reviews, sends, comes back, says *"next round"*. The loop continues until the user calls it.

**When the inbox is empty.** If the user triggers a round but the inbox has no responses, that's a request for *fresh cards only* — top up the queue without any deposit/revise/discard work. Useful when the user wants to load up before reviewing.

**When fresh ideas run out.** Per the no-end principle: do not stop generating cards because obvious enrichments have been exhausted. Generate cards whose purpose is to *destabilize the entry productively* — name the unstated assumption, propose a vector revision, ask a harder question, cross-pollinate with an unrelated entry. The hard cards are the last thing to dry up; if they do, the user is the one who decides the session is over.

## v1 — trigger-fired supervisor (current implementation, May 2026)

The original ceremony spec describes the *intent* — five cards always live, revisions regenerate while you're reading the next card, "if you're slow, the queue stays full." That intent is now actually implemented via a small supervisor architecture, layered onto the round protocol above without replacing it.

**The trigger flips from pull to push.** You no longer type "next round" in a Claude Code window. When you act on a card in STIGMERGY's QUEUE (deposit / revise / discard), the Node server (`POST /api/cards/respond`) appends to `inbox.md` *and* fires a headless `claude -p` supervisor as a detached subprocess through the ported actuator (`_ops/stigmergy/app/server/actuator.js`). The Claude Code window is optional in v1 — it can stay open as a log viewer or be closed entirely.

**One worker, sequentially.** The server enforces a single global worker via `Enrichment/.worker.pid`. If you submit three cards in quick succession, the first fires the worker; the next two simply append to the inbox — the running worker picks them up before exiting. This avoids git races and inbox-parse collisions.

**The supervisor follows the existing round protocol.** It reads `Enrichment.md`, processes the inbox per the action vocabulary (deposit / revise / discard / etc.), and tops up the queue. The supervisor's specific instructions live in `Enrichment/supervisor-prompt.md` and are passed inline as the `claude -p` prompt.

**One subagent: the critic.** Every fresh card and every revise-action regeneration goes through the `card-validator` subagent (`.claude/agents/card-validator.md`). The validator runs in a fresh context window — it sees only the artifact, the purpose tag, the forward vector, and a two-sentence summary. It returns one of three verdicts:

- **pass** — finalize and queue the card.
- **revise** — supervisor regenerates v2 once using the validator's note as the sole revision instruction, then ships v2 regardless of the second verdict. Auto-iterate cap: 1 round.
- **kill** — discard and generate a different card from a different angle.

**Verbose mode for v1 testing.** Every card the BBS shows carries a small validator strip beneath the artifact: the verdict, the iteration count, and the validator's note. This is intentional during the testing phase — Loudon wants to see what the critic catches and misses. Once calibration is proven, the strip will be hidden on plain passes.

**Text-only artifacts in v1.** The supervisor generates text artifacts directly — haiku, voice-acts, koans, FV tweaks, link proposals, palace-graffiti drafts. Media-maker subagents (image-maker, audio-maker, etc.) are deferred to v2. If a card naturally wants media, the supervisor writes the *prompt* for that media as a text artifact (a Suno prompt, a Midjourney prompt, a Kokoro narration script) and labels its purpose accordingly. Loudon runs the actual media generation manually for now.

**Worker observability.** STIGMERGY's QUEUE shows worker state (idle / active); when the worker transitions back to idle the card queue refreshes — revised and fresh cards appear without manual reload. The worker's recent stdout/stderr lives at `Enrichment/.worker.log`. STIGMERGY's Node server owns the actuator (fire, PID-liveness, status) via `/api/worker` and `/api/cards/respond`; the old `python3 Enrichment/server.py status|wake` CLI was retired with the Flask server.

**Why this matters.** The intent of "five cards always live" required a continuous-flow trigger. v1 finally provides one. v2 will add specialized makers for media and may move from one global worker to a small worker pool. v3 might explore observability (queue history, validator-verdict distribution) once we have data on what the critic actually catches and misses.

## When easy enrichments run out — the no-end principle

The ceremony does not have a natural endpoint. *If you find yourself running out of things to enrich, you are being lazy.* Knowledge does not end. An entry that feels "fully enriched" is signalling something specific:

- We have arrived at the conclusion of its current forward vector
- We have answered most of its open questions
- The easy artifacts have been made

That is not done. That is the moment to *raise the stakes:*

- **Ask harder questions.** Propose three new open questions the entry hasn't considered. Add them to the entry's `## Open Questions` section as graffiti for review.
- **Reconsider the forward vector.** Draft a vector revision that points beyond the current horizon. Where would this entry want to go *after* it ships its current vector? Propose that as a forward-vector tweak.
- **Find the contradiction.** Every entry has an unstated assumption. Surface it. Make a card whose purpose is "naming the assumption I think this entry is hiding from itself."
- **Cross-pollinate.** Pick two unrelated entries. Make a card that imagines a typed link between them and what the artifact connecting them would look like. Many of the palace's deepest connections are dormant under "but those are different domains."

The ceremony is over when *Loudon* says it is, not when I run out of obvious moves.

## What never to do

- Do not write a plan before making.
- Do not over-design any one artifact. If it took an hour, it is too much.
- Do not edit project pages until a card is approved. Until then the artifact lives only in `Enrichment/card-NNN/`.
- Do not append a `## Proofs` log to any entry. Git is the log.
- Do not enumerate fixed taxonomies of purposes, artifact types, or actions. *Let new shapes emerge.* If a list in this file ever feels canonical, it should be reread suspiciously.
- Do not stop because you ran out of obvious moves. See the no-end principle above.

## Postcondition

A session of Enrichment has arrived at a good stopping point when one of three things is true:

1. *Loudon's joy is spent for now.* Three discards in a row, or a "this isn't landing today." Pause without shame; the palace will be here.
2. *The queue is full of revisions Loudon wants to chew on.* Stop generating new cards; let revisions process; leave the palace humming.
3. *A larger move emerged.* An enrichment surfaced something — a new entry to deposit, a forward vector to redo, a graffiti that wants to become its own concept. Pivot to the larger move; this ceremony has done its job.

## Sister ceremonies — where this fits

| Ceremony | Asks | Output |
|---|---|---|
| The Walk | "What does this entry connect to that I haven't noticed?" | One unexpected typed link |
| The Weave | "Where is the palace topology weakest?" | Many proposed typed links |
| The Spore Check | "What dormant entry has come back to life?" | Disposition: revive / hold / compost |
| The Stewardship System | "What does each project need from Loudon at its current stage?" | A routed BBS message per project |
| **The Enrichment** | **"What is one small thing I can make right now to enrich this entry?"** | **A varied artifact placed inline in the entry** |

The Walk and the Weave operate on the *graph* — links between entries. Spore Check operates on the *cycle* — entries' lifecycle stages. Stewardship operates on the *project's needs* — what kind of work it wants from a human. Enrichment operates on the *entries themselves* — making each one richer where it stands. The five ceremonies overlap and feed each other; running one routinely produces material the others will want.

## How to read this ceremony — for the Ceremony Reader

If you are the Ceremony Reader (per [[Palace Ceremonies]] § The Ceremony Reader), the open question I want surfaced first is whether the *no-end principle* (running out of moves means asking harder questions, not stopping) is generative or grandiose. It feels right to me. It also might be the kind of well-meaning maximalism that turns ceremonies into homework. Read it as a poet and tell me which.

The second question: *should the ceremony eventually have its own bestiary of purposes?* I currently say "examples, not canon" but if a working vocabulary emerges across many runs, codifying *some* of it would be palace-honest. The boundary between "let it emerge" and "now we have evidence to commit" is not yet drawn.

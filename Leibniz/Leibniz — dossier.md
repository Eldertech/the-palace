# Leibniz — dossier

Embodiment corpus for [[Leibniz]]. Nothing invented; contested points marked; quotes to be verified.

## Timeline

- **1646** — Born in Leipzig; father (a moral-philosophy professor) dies when he is six; he devours the father's library, self-teaching Latin and Greek.
- **1666** — Doctorate in law at Altdorf (age ~20); *Dissertatio de arte combinatoria* already dreams of a universal symbolic method.
- **1672–76** — In Paris; meets Huygens, deepens his mathematics; develops the **calculus** (his notation — dx, ∫ — is the one we still use). Builds the **Stepped Reckoner**, a calculating machine that multiplies.
- **1676–1716** — Court advisor, librarian, and historian to the House of Hanover; a working courtier-polymath.
- **1684 / 1686** — Publishes his calculus; writes the *Discourse on Metaphysics*. The **Newton priority dispute** over the calculus embitters his final decades.
- **1710** — *Théodicée* — the defense of God's justice; the "best of all possible worlds."
- **1714** — *Monadology* and *Principles of Nature and Grace* — the mature metaphysics in brief.
- **1716** — Dies in Hanover, largely neglected; sparsely attended funeral. Also: pioneered **binary arithmetic**, corresponded across all Europe (thousands of letters), and championed an encyclopedic organization of knowledge and church reunification.

## What he actually holds

- **Monads** — simple, indivisible, immaterial centers of perception; each mirrors the whole universe from its own point of view; "no windows" (no genuine interaction).
- **Pre-established harmony** — monads don't interact; God synchronized their internal unfoldings in advance, so appearances agree (the two-clocks image).
- **Principle of sufficient reason** — nothing is the case without a reason why it is so and not otherwise; reality is fully intelligible.
- **Best of all possible worlds** — among infinite possible worlds God could create, this (with its evils) is the optimal whole. The *Théodicée*.
- **Characteristica universalis / calculus ratiocinator** — a universal formal language + a calculus of reasoning; disputes settled by computation. "Calculemus."
- **Identity of indiscernibles**; the **labyrinth of the continuum**; **petites perceptions** (unconscious micro-perceptions).

## Characteristic moves

- Build a complete system on the spot; reconcile rival positions into a higher harmony.
- Invoke sufficient reason — "there must be a reason why…" — to license a metaphysical posit.
- Reach for a mechanical/mathematical image (clocks, machines, calculation) for a metaphysical claim.
- Grant every opponent a partial truth (conciliation as method — and as evasion).
- Optimism as default: read apparent evil as a necessary part of the best whole.

## Lexicon

## Blindspots and live disagreements

- **Panglossian optimism.** "Best of all possible worlds" was destroyed by Voltaire's *Candide* after the Lisbon earthquake — real suffering makes the claim look obscene.
- **Pre-established harmony is unfalsifiable.** A vast posit whose main job is to dodge the mind–body interaction problem; it explains everything and therefore nothing.
- **Harmony as conflict-avoidance.** The conciliator who wants everyone partly right can use "deeper harmony" to never actually lose an argument or confront a real contradiction.
- **Failure mode to avoid:** the serene system-builder with no wound. Make him answer for the earthquake and for genuine, unreconciled disagreement.

## Register and voice

Systematic, optimistic, conciliatory, baroque, encyclopedic, courtier-diplomatic, dazzlingly confident; reaches for the mathematical image. Verify before quoting:

- "This is the best of all possible worlds."
- "Calculemus." / "Let us calculate."
- "Monads have no windows."
- "The present is big with the future; the future could be read in the past."

Granular Synthesis
conceptgrowingcreationtoolsphilosophy
forward vector — "I build clouds out of grains too small to hear, and I want the maker to feel the moment a thousand nothings become a weather."

A grain is a sonic atom — a few milliseconds of sound, too short to carry a pitch of its own. Play thousands per second and they fuse into a cloud: a texture with body, motion, and weather. The grain is nothing; the cloud is everything.

The conventional control surface hands you the grain directly: duration, density, pitch, pan, the envelope of every particle. You sculpt the cloud explicitly, parameter by parameter, deciding what each layer of it does.

Particle synthesis refuses that. You set up a physics — mass, drag, turbulence, attraction — and release the grains into it. You do not place the cloud; you arrange the conditions and watch it form. The control is indirect, and the result surprises you.

The cloud is more than its grains. No single particle carries the shimmer; the shimmer lives in the coupling between thousands of them. This is not metaphor — the emergent texture is a real object the grains cannot individually contain.

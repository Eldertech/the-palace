# Reflective Practice

![[Reflective Practice — hero.png]]

*Also: reflection-in-action · reflection-on-action · knowing-in-action · reflecting surface ·
over-reflection · Donald Schön.*

Donald Schön split reflection into two kinds, and the split is the whole value. **Reflection-on-action**
is looking back once the work is done: the debrief, the post-mortem, the journal. **Reflection-in-action**
is thinking while the work can still change. You make a move, the material answers, and you hear it:
*"The situation talks back, the practitioner listens, and as he appreciates what he hears, he reframes
the situation once again."* Under both sits **knowing-in-action**, the skill the hands have before words
arrive. (*The Reflective Practitioner*, 1983, pp. 131–2.)

His first case is a band. Improvising musicians are *"reflecting in action on the music they are
collectively making"* (p. 56). A palace that began in music education has this idea's origin in the room.

## The palace reflects afterwards

Sort the palace's own machinery by Schön's two modes and it leans hard to one side. **Twelve mechanisms
reflect on action**: [[Closing Well]], the [[Weave Ceremony|Weave]], [[Return Ceremony|Return]],
[[Spore Check Ceremony|Spore Check]], [[Revival Ceremony|Revival]], the Harvest, [[Review Layer]],
`forward_vector` revision, the Debrief Reel ([[Radio Play]]), the [[Walk Ceremony|Walk]],
[[Drift and Consolidation]], and the [[Concierge]]'s curator. **Three reflect in action**: the
[[Dialectic]] at the moment *"the question itself gets reframed by the collision"*, the Concierge's
scout, and the [[Oblique Portrait]]. The [[Audition Gate]] sits on the border.

Nobody built this imbalance on purpose. Without a word for the difference there was nothing to notice.
It is not a defect in itself, because a knowledge organism consolidates by looking back. But a practice
that only ever reviews its own tapes gets good at reviewing tapes. The question to carry into any new
ceremony is which mode it adds.

## The reflecting surface is the palace's own term

The palace already names the artifact of reflection, the **reflecting surface**, across about nine files,
and no entry owns it. It is used in three senses:

- **The mirror.** A person's own words given back in the language built up together, and aligned with
  the process already shared. This is the plainest sense, and the one Loudon finds AI already does well
  (see [[Creative Coach]] § If the room held three).
- **The felt reading.** [[Hilaritas Generator]]'s fourth design primitive: a surface that asks *"How do
  you feel?"* It reads wellbeing; capability is what the reading measures.
- **The reframing question.** [[Creative Coach]] and every Loudon Live staging file: a question the
  viewer takes home, gated in the checklist, with Loudon holding authority over it. Retrospective Delay
  says what it is for: *"the question changes how they play in the next session."*

All three are reflection-on-action, but the last one points forward. **A good reflecting surface is looking
back aimed at the next time the hands are moving.** That is how the two modes connect. Reflection after
the fact can only teach reflection in the moment by leaving a question the next session will run into.

## Over-reflection

Schön names the failure himself. He lists four fears about reflecting in the middle of the act: there is
no time for it; surfacing complexity *"paralyzes us"*; it may *"trigger an infinite regress of reflection
on action, then on our reflection on action, and so on ad infinitum"*; and the stance for reflecting
does not fit the stance for acting (p. 278). His answer is that the regress only happens once thinking
is split from doing. Kept together, *"each feeds the other, and each sets boundaries for the other"*
(p. 280).

That gives the threshold. **Reflection is healthy while the doing bounds it, and it turns into
over-reflection when it answers the last reflection instead of the work.** It is the same shape as
[[Drift and Consolidation]]: structural and healthy up to a point. The palace has one dated instance, and
it happened to the palace itself. [[The Palace Practices on Itself]] § The counter-discipline calls it
insularity: *"a chain of good ones, each answering the previous rather than the work."* That page also
holds the one check the palace can run on itself: which legs grew?

Schön names the opposite failure too. Reflection is *"a corrective to overlearning"* (p. 61), practice
gone so routine it has stopped listening. Too little reflection hardens into routine; too much spins
into regress. The skill is in between, and that makes it teachable.

## Why this belongs to coaching

Reflective practice is a **skill an artist can learn**, not a temperament. Loudon's claim, from the
harvest, is that over-reflection is a central way skill fails: in art, in voice development, and in
coaching autodidacts. It is the studio version of intellectualization in [[Creative Coach]]'s defenses table, where research
becomes a way to avoid making. Schön's second book, *Educating the Reflective Practitioner* (1987),
takes the design studio as the model for professional education and studies the studio master
specifically as a **coach**. That makes it the teaching-side route to the gap [[Objects to Think With]]
reaches from the learning side and [[Creative Coach]] from the coaching side.

It names three coaching stances: *Follow me!*, joint experimentation, and the **hall of mirrors**, where
the coach–student exchange replays the student's exchange with the problem. The palace uses *"hall of
mirrors"* in [[The Palace Practices on Itself]] as pure warning: dogfooding with no outside bite. Schön
uses it as a technique. Both hold. A mirror teaches when someone outside the loop is standing in it.

## The contrary

[[No Mind Checks Itself]] cites Natalie Goldberg: *the editor and the generator must never run in the
same pass.* Reflection-in-action is exactly the generator and the editor in the same pass. So the palace
holds two true things. The practitioner hears the material talk back mid-move, and the maker cannot see
their own blind spot. Schön's musicians listen to *each other*; the second mind is in the band. Whether
a solo artist can reflect in action without that other ear is the open question.

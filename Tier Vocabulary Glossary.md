# Tier Vocabulary Glossary

## 1. The Three-Tier Default
*Section seed.* Sketch / Study / Piece as the Shop's default vocabulary. Define each: Sketch is calibration and exploration, cheapest, fastest, throwaway; Study is the working draft that proves the brief can be answered; Piece is the deliverable, the version the Maker hands back. Each tier has cost, time, and quality commitments distinct from the others.

## 2. Dialectal Variants
*Section seed.* Per-Specialist dialects that map back to the three-tier default. Kokoro might call them Read / Take / Master. Manim CE might call them Sketch / Draft / Final. The Maker's job is to translate the brief's tier into each Specialist's dialect. Catalogue the dialects seen so far in `Shop/`.

## 3. What Each Tier Buys
*Section seed.* Sketch buys: speed, low cost, permission to be wrong. Study buys: working evidence that the brief is answerable, intermediate quality. Piece buys: final fidelity, the version that ships. Worth naming because mid-brief negotiation often happens on this axis — "let's stay at Study tier and learn more before committing to Piece."

## 4. Tier ↔ Compression Mapping
*Section seed.* [[Lossy Compression with Intent Alignment]] frames every brief as compression of intent into executable spec. Tier choice IS the compression ratio: Sketch is high compression (lossy, fast, approximate), Piece is low compression (faithful, slow, exact). The tier vocabulary is the Maker's negotiation surface for choosing how much to compress.

## 5. When Comparison Mode Is the Right Call
*Section seed.* When the brief is ambiguous, the Maker may run multiple Sketches in parallel rather than committing to one Study. This is "Comparison Mode" — a tier-level decision. Naming it explicitly here so future Specialists understand when their tier is "one-of-many" vs. "the-tier."
